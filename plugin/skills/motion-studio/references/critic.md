# The critic pass

The blind read asks whether the film still makes its point. The critic asks whether it is worth watching. Run it on every long-form draft and on any short film the user calls dull.

## 1. Numbers first

`longform.py check <film>` measures the render and the timeline:

- **text-only share**: time spent on words over a background (`title chapter statement quote list`). Over 40% flags; so does a text-only run over 25 s, or three scenes of one type in a row. A scene of one of those types counts only if it really is words: check probes the frame on a 64×36 grid at 30% and 60% through the scene and once it has settled, and if shapes other than text (not see-through, not frame-sized) are on top at `--picture-min` (6%) of the probes or more, the scene is reported as drawn as a picture and left out. The engine's own text scenes measure 0–3%, so a kit pattern that turns a statement into motion graphics passes without relabelling it (Longtok had to relabel two as `stat`, 2026-10-24). `--picture-min 0` goes back to judging by type alone. Trade-offs: a picture made of thin lines (a track, a hairline chart) covers few probes and still counts as text, and the three-in-a-row rule still goes by type.
- **holds**: stretches where nothing visible changes (mean luma change under the `--event` threshold) for `--hold` seconds (default 4). A 10-minute explainer should have none over 6 s; the original toll-text cut had 59 over 4 s.
- **per chapter**: events per minute and the share of still frames, so a flat chapter stands out from the others.
- **cue landings**: every word cue should produce a visible change within -0.2 to +0.8 s of its word. A change in any small block above the caption band counts (`--local`), so a small reveal such as a spoke label registers.
- **pop diagnosis** (`scripts/popdiag.py <film> <frame,frame,...>`): for frames final's pop check names, the same frame measured in out/silent.mp4, out/silent-raw.mp4, a page drawing it in sequence and a fresh page, raw and finished, with the render chunk it falls in. A pop only in silent.mp4 is the finish, one in the videos but in neither page run is the chunk join or the encode, and fresh against sequence above 0 is the page's own state. Each video frame's mean level against the page's names which side of a step is off. At a chunk's first frame it also reads the two chunk files on their own, and it encodes the page's frames around k the way render does (lossless chunks, one final encode) with this machine's ffmpeg, as one chunk and cut at k: a step only in the cut encode is the encoder. On the first cel render this found the cause: chunks that were each a lossy encode stepped 1.1 to 1.3 levels at a cut against 0.1 to 0.35 as one encode. Prints a table and saves PNGs to review/pops/. Render settings that change the picture path (render.py, finish.py, the chunk encoder, MS_FRAME_FORMAT) are in the render stamp, so a render after an engine pull starts fresh and `--only` refuses.
- **beat landings** (`scripts/cuecheck.py`; `check` and `final`): the same cues, plus every cut between segments, timed on the rendered video at 30 fps. For each one it finds the frame where the picture starts to change (the first clear change within -0.3 to +0.6 s of the word, measured per 12x12 block at 384x216 after taking away each block's own motion, its 90th percentile over the half second before, so a camera or sparkle on twos doesn't count and a chart's start dot does) and reports how far that lands from the word. A clear change more than 0.25 s early or 0.2 s late is flagged in `check` and fails `final`. A cue with no clear change, or one under 0.35 s after another, is counted and not judged; so is one whose first change comes on the next cue's word, since that change is the next cue's landing (a cut into a held picture whose first mark is cued on its first word). `final` also lines the finished video's sound up with `out/mix.wav` and fails beyond 45 ms. `check` skips the timing when `out/silent.mp4` is older than timeline.js. This catches drift in the render, the chunk joins or the mux, which the page checks can't see. In the render-layer test (2026-11-28) it read every flip in the Survive Year 1 intro within a frame of its word, and it flagged all 18 cues when the picture was shifted 0.3 s late. On the first cel render it called a write-on in a scene moving on twos 312 ms early and a chart's start dot 257 ms late (timed on the line drawn after it); both were on their words, and the per-block, first-clear-change measure reads them right. Trade-off: the first clear change can be a small unrelated mark that happens near the word, which reads as on time.
- **one-frame flashes**: a frame that differs from both neighbours while they match each other (back-to-back pops that verify's single-frame check misses).
- **flash rate** (WCAG 2.3.1, general flash; 2026-11-22): no more than 3 flashes in any one second over a quarter of a 10-degree field. A flash is a pair of opposing changes in relative luminance of at least 0.1 where the darker state is under 0.8. Every frame of the render is read at 96 px wide; the field is taken as a third of the frame's width (4:3), which is the 341x256 block WCAG gives for a 1024x768 screen. It is a flag, not a failure, while it is tuned. `--no-flash-rate` skips it; `scripts/readcheck.py flash <video>` runs it on any video. Trade-offs: the red-flash rule (saturated red) is not tested. On a phone held upright a 10-degree field covers most of the screen, so the third-of-the-width field is stricter than a phone viewer needs. A flash in a patch under about 1/100 of the frame is averaged away at 96 px, but that is far under the area limit anyway.
- **text size**: every piece of reading text on screen, measured in the page at the end of each segment and just after each word cue, after the scene's camera. Under `--min-text` (56 px at 1920 wide, scaled to the frame's width: 32 px in a 9:16 Short) flags. A 16:9 video in a phone held upright is about 6.5 cm wide, so 56 px is about 11 pt; the owner watches on phones and asked for the information to be bigger (2026-10-23). Labels a viewer need not read (chapter and source lines, kickers, the sender line, attributions) carry `data-aux` in the template and are exempt. It needs no render, only `timeline`.
- **page**: runs first. A JavaScript error, `console.error` or unhandled rejection at any segment's start, middle or end stops the check with a list of each one, and blocks `render` until a check passes on that version of the film. See "The page gate" in longform.md.
- **overlap**: text that something is painted over, or two pieces of text on top of each other. This came after toll-text at the 56 px floor had 36 collisions in 9 segments (cards over cards, stamps over words, a highlight over a notification; 2026-10-24). Every visible piece of text is probed on a 15×5 grid inside its box with the browser's own hit test, which follows the real glyph shapes. Anything painted above the text at two or more probes counts. So does other text at those probes, whichever is on top. Shapes under 0.15 opacity, shapes larger than 60% of the frame (vignette, grade, wipes) and shapes marked `data-over` (compare's strike-through) are skipped. Samples are taken at the text-size moments, 0.3 s after each of them, and every `--overlap-step` seconds (0.25) through each segment, so a card that crosses words on its way in is caught as well as one that lands on them. A collision has to be seen at two samples at most 0.35 s apart, so a stamp slamming down past a word is not reported. `--overlap-step 0` keeps only the settled moments, and `-1` skips the test. It takes about a minute on a 10-minute film and needs no render. Text that lands inside a visible `data-clear` element's box (a hand-built stage layer marked keep-clear, below or above the text) fails the same way, unless the text belongs to that element. Trade-offs: a collision that lasts under about a quarter second is never reported. Two probes on a long line of text is a small share, so it reports a thin stroke across a word, which is the intent, and occasionally the edge of a deliberate highlight; mark that shape `data-over`.
- **edge**: reading text cut off at the frame edge, or on an upright Short reaching outside the safe area (`SAFE`: where YouTube's buttons and caption bar cover the picture). This came after five toll-text scenes had labels half off the frame, one of them for 14 s (2026-10-24). The visible-text filter is the same as for overlap, and the box has 2 px of slack. Labels (`data-aux`: source lines, kickers) are held to the frame only, since the template sets some in a Short's margin on purpose. Text marked `data-edge` (a ticker meant to run off the side) is skipped, and text wholly off the frame is unseen, so it is not reported. It is sampled with the overlap test and judged the same way, so text sliding in from off-frame is not reported and anything that stays is. `--overlap-step -1` skips both. Trade-off: the test uses the text's box, so a tilted line whose box corner pokes past the edge while its glyphs stay inside is reported.
- **determinism**: the same moment rendered twice must give the same pixels; `--repeat` moments (8) are rendered again in the same page and in a fresh browser, and any difference fails with its share of pixels. See "Repeatable frames" in longform.md.
- **text hold**: every block of reading text a viewer must take in (scene text, bubbles, cards; not captions or labels) must stay up long enough to read once it is fully shown. `check` samples the film every `--text-step` seconds (0.1; 0 skips) and writes `review/text-sheet.tsv`: each block, when it is fully shown in its final state, how long it holds, what it needs, and whether that passes. A block needs max(2 s, 1 s + words / 3); at least 3 s when its segment has `"payoff": true` or a kit marks it `data-payoff`, or when it is still up on the last frame before a chapter cut. Text that only ever appears moving or half revealed is "never fully shown" and fails. This came from the owner's preview note that a reveal moved on before he could take it in (2026-10-26). Fully shown means at least 0.95 opacity after its parents, at least 98% inside its rise mask and its scene's wipe, the same string and size, and moving under 60 px/s in its scene's own coordinates, so a camera move or a bubble bobbing with its speaker still counts as held. The lines of one wrapped block (same segment and size, stacked, overlapping sideways, settling within 1 s and leaving within 0.3 s of each other) are read as one block, from its last line's settle. Trade-offs: times are to the nearest sample (0.1 s). A count-up number counts only from its last value. Two separate texts that happen to sit stacked and arrive together are read as one block, which asks for more time than either needs. The engine's rule (about 0.33 s a word plus 1 s, at least 2 s) is stricter than the 0.3 s a word and 1 s minimum in raea's craft floor, so it stays.
- **text contrast** (2026-11-22): every block in the text sheet must reach `--contrast` (3:1, WCAG's ratio for large text; all reading text is at least 56 px at 1920 wide) against the pixels actually behind it, not against the theme's colour. At 3 moments through each block's hold (its settle, the middle and the end), check takes a picture of the block's lines with the text drawn and again with it hidden. The pixels that change are the text, the ones that change most are its core (so antialiased edges don't count), and the core is split into glyphs at blank columns. A glyph's contrast is the median ratio of the text's colour to the colour behind it over its core pixels, and the block's is its worst glyph at its worst moment. So a word half over a bright patch of a dark photo is caught by the glyphs on the patch. The text sheet gets a `contrast` column and a `readable` column: the hold times the share of moments that pass. Its `ok` uses the readable time, and a block under the ratio is flagged once, with how long it was readable. `--contrast 0` skips it. Trade-offs: 3 moments per block, so a background that passes under the text between them is missed. A text with its own outline or plate is measured against what's behind the whole drawing (the engine's text has neither; a kit that adds a plate should draw it as a separate element, which then counts as the ground). It adds about 4 s per 10 blocks (8 s for the 20 blocks of the demo film, so a 10-minute film with 300 blocks takes about 2 minutes more). A stream with a stricter rule (4.5:1) passes `--contrast 4.5`.
- **still picture**: covered already. `final` runs verify's motion gate (motioncheck), which fails any 2 s stretch whose motion is under the floor and any still 0.5 s in the first 3 s. That is stricter than raea's 3 s, so check adds no separate test. The `--hold` count here (4 s with no visible event) is about nothing new happening, not stillness.

- **dead air**: the voice stops for more than `--dead-air` seconds (1.5; 0 skips) while 3 words or fewer of reading text are on screen and nothing moves faster than an idle. It came from the owner's note on a pause after a single number with nothing to take in (2026-10-26). Fix it by giving the pause something to look at (a reveal, a reaction, a camera move) or by cutting the pause (`extra`, `hold`).
- **pose variety**: a pose key that repeats the key before it on the same figure (in stage beats or a kit's own F.at or F.closeup at) is a beat that changes nothing. A figure that only idles, breathing and swaying, while the voice speaks for more than `--idle-figure` seconds (3; 0 skips) of the stretch is flagged too. Background people (F.crowd) are left out. A stage figure is named by its cast id; other figures get a name from `opts.id`, else their x position.
- **beat length**: a stretch in which nothing new starts moving or enters while the voice speaks for more than `--beat` seconds of it (5; 0 skips). It came from about 17 s of voice over one factory loop. A loop that keeps going is not new, and neither is a figure that only idles; something entering or showing, a colour or text change, a figure starting a new move, or the camera starting to move is new. Mark a scene meant to hold with `data-hold` on any element, or `"steady": true` on its segment, and beats with it on screen aren't flagged.
- **pace profile** (report only, never a flag): the share of time something moves, the median time between shots (segments), the median and 90th-percentile time between new things (new starts within 0.5 s count as one), narration words a second over the time the voice runs, and sound effects per 10 s (read from `out/sfx_events.json`, which `mix` writes). Each number sits beside a fast-cut benchmark: an X article on viral 2.5D stickman channels (caesarscales, 2026-11-24), whose author counted about 33,000 frames from 20 videos of one channel. He found something moving in 96.6% of frames, a new picture about every 2.1 s, something new about every 1 s, 3.67 words a second with pauses cut, and about 8 sound effects per 10 s. These are his numbers and are unverified. The benchmark is for comparison, because it measures a short, fast format: our long-form rules hold a picture 5 to 8 s (picture-truth.md), leave about 0.3 s extra before a transition, and keep narration at a slower pace, on purpose. A Short is the place to aim near it. Ideas from that article already in the engine: an action on the narrator's word (cue landings), a contact sheet before render (`stills`), a moving background (the motion floor), and a visual for every noun (critic). The article's kit says nothing about its licence, so none of its code is used.
- How the pacing tests measure: the same `--text-step` pass as the text sheet (0 skips them all) installs a probe that records each figure's pose and placement and every change to the drawing's attributes and text. Breathing and sway run at about 8 degree-like units a second, and a figure counts as moving above 20. An element counts as moving when its box moves faster than 20 px/s or its opacity changes faster than 0.2 a second. It counts as entering when it first changes, shows, hides, or changes colour or text. Figures' own parts, `<defs>` and anything under 32 x 32 px (the background dots) are left out. The voice is the aligned word times (a narrated segment with no words counts as speaking to its end less the pad), with pauses under 0.6 s bridged for "while the voice speaks". Trade-offs: these are flags for judgement, not failures, because a held pause or a long take can be the point; expect some to be kept. The probe reads what the page changes, not the pixels, so a change hidden behind something or under a mask still counts as motion and can hide a flag; a kit that captured F.at before check loads isn't seen by the repeated-key test. The pass takes about 30% longer than the text sheet alone (9 s against 7 s on a 53 s film).
- **icons**: every `data-icon` (draw them with `K.icon`, longform.md, Icons and the end screen at phone size) is at least `--icon-min` px (18) on its longer side with the frame 360 px wide. Look at `review/endscreen-360.png` too: at that size each symbol must be recognisable, and a share icon is a curved arrow, never a lone chevron.
- **brand**: every logo a kit marks `data-logo` is drawn in the channel's palette (longform.md, The channel logo keeps its colours): a flag in `check`, a failure in `final`. A fail means a style, grade or vignette is drawn over the logo, or the kit draws it in other colours; the message gives the typical wrong colour and how far it is from the nearest palette colour.
- **joins**: the voice gap into every transition segment (a new place, subject or chapter; longform.md, Transitions breathe) must be at least the film's usual continuation gap plus `--transition-hold` (0.3 s; 0 skips). `timeline` adds the hold, so a flag means a hand edit, an `extra` or a kit change undid it. Fix it with `extra` on the segment before, or mark the segment `"join": "continue"` if it carries on the same place and subject.

## 2. A fresh critic, not the builder

Give a fresh agent only: the contact sheet (`stills --every 2.5`), `review/check.txt`, the script's narration, and the brief. Never the builder's reasoning or notes. Prompt:

> You are reviewing a draft explainer video. You see stills every 2.5 s, the narration, and measured numbers. Report only problems you can point to in these materials; if a section has none, say so and do not invent one. For each chapter: the seconds it could lose, any stretch where the picture repeats what was just shown, any number on screen without a source, and two questions. Shown: when the narration says something happens or works, is that thing on screen, doing it, at that moment? Name each line where it is only talked about. Pull: does each scene end on something unresolved (a question, a missing piece, a change not yet explained) that the next scene answers? Name each scene that ends closed. Then the top 6 fixes, most important first, each tied to a segment id and a timestamp.

## 3. Verify the fixes

After changes, a second fresh critic gets the previous list and the new materials and marks each item FIXED, PARTLY or STILL PRESENT, then answers SHIP or ONE MORE PASS (at most 3 more fixes). Fix one thing at a time and keep timing and motion otherwise untouched, so a revision doesn't regress what worked.
