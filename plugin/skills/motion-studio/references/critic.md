# The critic pass

The blind read asks whether the film still makes its point. The critic asks whether it is worth watching. Run it on every long-form draft and on any short film the user calls dull.

## 1. Numbers first

`longform.py check <film>` measures the render and the timeline:

- **text-only share**: time spent on words over a background (`title chapter statement quote list`). Over 40% flags; so does a text-only run over 25 s, or three scenes of one type in a row. A scene of one of those types counts only if it really is words: check probes the frame on a 64×36 grid at 30% and 60% through the scene and once it has settled, and if shapes other than text (not see-through, not frame-sized) are on top at `--picture-min` (6%) of the probes or more, the scene is reported as drawn as a picture and left out. The engine's own text scenes measure 0–3%, so a kit pattern that turns a statement into motion graphics passes without relabelling it (Longtok had to relabel two as `stat`, 2026-10-24). `--picture-min 0` goes back to judging by type alone. Trade-offs: a picture made of thin lines (a track, a hairline chart) covers few probes and still counts as text, and the three-in-a-row rule still goes by type.
- **holds**: stretches where nothing visible changes (mean luma change under the `--event` threshold) for `--hold` seconds (default 4). A 10-minute explainer should have none over 6 s; the original toll-text cut had 59 over 4 s.
- **per chapter**: events per minute and the share of still frames, so a flat chapter stands out from the others.
- **cue landings**: every word cue should produce a visible change within -0.2 to +0.8 s of its word. A change in any small block above the caption band counts (`--local`), so a small reveal such as a spoke label registers.
- **one-frame flashes**: a frame that differs from both neighbours while they match each other (back-to-back pops that verify's single-frame check misses).
- **text size**: every piece of reading text on screen, measured in the page at the end of each segment and just after each word cue, after the scene's camera. Under `--min-text` (56 px at 1920 wide, scaled to the frame's width: 32 px in a 9:16 Short) flags. A 16:9 video in a phone held upright is about 6.5 cm wide, so 56 px is about 11 pt; the owner watches on phones and asked for the information to be bigger (2026-10-23). Labels a viewer need not read (chapter and source lines, kickers, the sender line, attributions) carry `data-aux` in the template and are exempt. It needs no render, only `timeline`.
- **overlap**: text that something is painted over, or two pieces of text on top of each other. This came after toll-text at the 56 px floor had 36 collisions in 9 segments (cards over cards, stamps over words, a highlight over a notification; 2026-10-24). Every visible piece of text is probed on a 15×5 grid inside its box with the browser's own hit test, which follows the real glyph shapes. Anything painted above the text at two or more probes counts. So does other text at those probes, whichever is on top. Shapes under 0.15 opacity, shapes larger than 60% of the frame (vignette, grade, wipes) and shapes marked `data-over` (compare's strike-through) are skipped. Samples are taken at the text-size moments, 0.3 s after each of them, and every `--overlap-step` seconds (0.25) through each segment, so a card that crosses words on its way in is caught as well as one that lands on them. A collision has to be seen at two samples at most 0.35 s apart, so a stamp slamming down past a word is not reported. `--overlap-step 0` keeps only the settled moments, and `-1` skips the test. It takes about a minute on a 10-minute film and needs no render. Text that lands inside a visible `data-clear` element's box (a hand-built stage layer marked keep-clear, below or above the text) fails the same way, unless the text belongs to that element. Trade-offs: a collision that lasts under about a quarter second is never reported. Two probes on a long line of text is a small share, so it reports a thin stroke across a word, which is the intent, and occasionally the edge of a deliberate highlight; mark that shape `data-over`.
- **edge**: reading text cut off at the frame edge, or on an upright Short reaching outside the safe area (`SAFE`: where YouTube's buttons and caption bar cover the picture). This came after five toll-text scenes had labels half off the frame, one of them for 14 s (2026-10-24). The visible-text filter is the same as for overlap, and the box has 2 px of slack. Labels (`data-aux`: source lines, kickers) are held to the frame only, since the template sets some in a Short's margin on purpose. Text marked `data-edge` (a ticker meant to run off the side) is skipped, and text wholly off the frame is unseen, so it is not reported. It is sampled with the overlap test and judged the same way, so text sliding in from off-frame is not reported and anything that stays is. `--overlap-step -1` skips both. Trade-off: the test uses the text's box, so a tilted line whose box corner pokes past the edge while its glyphs stay inside is reported.
- **determinism**: the same moment rendered twice must give the same pixels; `--repeat` moments (8) are rendered again in the same page and in a fresh browser, and any difference fails with its share of pixels. See "Repeatable frames" in longform.md.

## 2. A fresh critic, not the builder

Give a fresh agent only: the contact sheet (`stills --every 2.5`), `review/check.txt`, the script's narration, and the brief. Never the builder's reasoning or notes. Prompt:

> You are reviewing a draft explainer video. You see stills every 2.5 s, the narration, and measured numbers. Report only problems you can point to in these materials; if a section has none, say so and do not invent one. For each chapter: the seconds it could lose, any stretch where the picture repeats what was just shown, any line whose picture doesn't show what is being said, and any number on screen without a source. Then the top 6 fixes, most important first, each tied to a segment id and a timestamp.

## 3. Verify the fixes

After changes, a second fresh critic gets the previous list and the new materials and marks each item FIXED, PARTLY or STILL PRESENT, then answers SHIP or ONE MORE PASS (at most 3 more fixes). Fix one thing at a time and keep timing and motion otherwise untouched, so a revision doesn't regress what worked.
