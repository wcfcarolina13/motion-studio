# Long-form narrated films

For 5–20 minute 16:9 explainers (YouTube). The narration is the clock: each segment of the script gets one voice clip, the clip's length sets the segment's length, and anything in a scene can be timed to a spoken word. Run everything with `L=${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/longform.py`.

## Pipeline

| Step | Command | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| scaffold | `python3 $L init <film>` | film.html (engine), kit.js, figure.js, closeup.js, folders |
| voice | Before voicing, the narration gets the spoken line edit (SKILL.md, step 11's note: the `spoken-line-edit` skill, then the claim audit again on changed lines, then the AI-tells check). `python3 $L lines <film>` → one mp3 per segment id in `narration/` (any TTS or a human read). It prints what the voice should say, with the `say` map applied, and lists likely misreads on stderr as `SAY:` lines; fix them before voicing. After voicing, `python3 $L voiced <film>` records each clip's voice, pack, speed, words and hash; `timeline` stops on a clip that is unrecorded, changed since, voiced for other words, or voiced with another voice, pack or speed | `narration/s01.mp3` …, `narration/voice.json` |
| word times | `python3 $L align <film>` | `narration/words.json` (offline forced alignment) |
| timeline | `python3 $L timeline <film>` | `timeline.js`, `out/narration.wav`, `out/chapters.txt`, `out/captions.vtt` (`captions --lang es --text es.json` adds `out/captions.es.vtt`) |
| look | `python3 $L stills <film> --every 2.5` | stills + contact sheet in `review/` |
| music | `python3 $L music <film>` then `python3 $L mix <film> --plan` then `python3 $L credits <film>` | `out/music_plan.json`, `out/mix.wav`, with UI sounds from the timeline (below; `--no-sfx` leaves them out) and the library's whoosh at chapter starts (`--no-whoosh`); `out/credits.txt` for the description. `mix --plan` stops when the plan no longer matches the timeline's chapters or end (run `music` again), and when the bed goes quiet (30 dB under its usual level) more than 3 s before the film ends; the last track runs under the tail and end screen. The bed's gain is set from its gap under the voice (16 LU for a Short, 18 LU long), and `mix` stops on a bed that is inaudible, crowds the voice or masks its consonants (sound.md, The bed under the voice) |
| check | `python3 $L check <film>` | `review/check.txt`, `review/text-sheet.tsv`, `review/check-gate.json` (see `critic.md`); render needs a passing page gate on this version of the film. Run it again after `render` for the dead-air, cue and flash tests |
| render | `python3 $L render <film> --workers 4` | `out/silent.mp4` |
| ship | `python3 $L final <film>` | `out/<film>.mp4`, verify report |
| upload | `python3 ${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/youtube.py upload … --privacy private` | private video; `captions` adds the VTT; going public and the 48-hour review: `publish.md` |
| share a draft | `python3 $L share <film>` | `out/share/`: a page that streams the cut, to publish as a private artifact (below) |
| Short | `python3 $L short <film> <short> --from s05 --to s09`, then lay it out, `timeline`, `stills`, `render`, `final` in the Short's folder | a 9:16 film that reuses the long-form voice and sound (below) |

`align` times each word from the audio and tries three methods in order. **Whisper** comes first. It is mlx-whisper on an Apple-silicon Mac (`pipx install mlx-whisper`; align finds the pipx venv, or the interpreter in `$MS_WHISPER_PY`) and faster-whisper elsewhere. The model is `$MS_WHISPER_MODEL`, default small, with MIT-licensed weights that are downloaded once and cached. Whisper's word timestamps are matched in order to the script's words, so the captions keep the script's text and only the times come from the audio. Both sides are compared as spoken words, so "$4,860" matches however Whisper wrote it. A word it misheard takes the gap between its neighbours, and a clip where it heard under 60% of the script is refused. **PocketSphinx** comes next (`pip install pocketsphinx`). **Spread** is the last resort: words placed by spoken weight between measured pauses. That is an estimate, off by up to 0.5 s on a long clip, and it moves every cue keyed to a word: callouts, map tags, cuts and counters. `align` prints which methods timed the film and tries a spread segment again on its next run. `check` flags every spread or unaligned narrated segment, and `render` refuses them (`--allow-spread` for a rough draft). Why: Survive Year 1 had 140 of 140 segments spread, because PocketSphinx was never installed on the Mac, and that was the cause of the owner's out-of-sync notes (2026-11-27). Numbers, money and years are expanded the way a narrator says them ("$4.12" → four dollars twelve cents) before aligning; words the dictionary doesn't know take the gap between their neighbours. A year is tried both as "twenty twenty-five" and "two thousand twenty-five" (voices differ), keeping the reading whose word starts land on the measured pauses, and word edges are then snapped onto measured silence (the aligner otherwise hands leading silence to the first word).

## script.json

```json
{"title": "…", "moods": {"Intro": "mystery", "The trick": "tension"}, "captions": false, "type_scale": 1,
 "segments": [
  {"id": "s01", "chapter": "Intro", "type": "stage", "source": "…", "narration": "You've probably had this text…",
   "data": {"set": "room", "cast": [{"id": "you", "x": 0.36, "skin": 2, "hair": "short", "shirt": "#e8772e"}],
            "beats": [{"who": "you", "at": "text", "pose": "phone", "glow": true, "notify": "TOLL NOTICE: …"},
                      {"who": "you", "at": "late fee", "pose": "read", "emote": "!"}]}}]}
```

**Text size.** Reading text in every scene type is at least `READ` (56 px wide, 40 px in a Short), so it holds up on a phone, and `check` fails anything smaller. `"type_scale": 1.2` scales that floor and the list size for one film. The list carries the advice, so its items are 66 px. A text message is drawn at a phone's proportions, so once it lands the camera pushes in (and holds still) until the message reads at the floor. A stage notification sits beside the phone hand at head height, or the nearest clear spot (lower, higher, the other side, overhead) that covers no one else's body, bubble or the kicker. Trade-offs: bigger text means fewer words per line, so long list items and board cards wrap onto more lines, and board cards are larger (440 px wide); a board with many cards needs more room between them. A board's stamp sits under its card's words, or over the card's top edge when the board ends first, and `check` fails any text that something is painted over (critic.md, overlap).

**Icons and the end screen at phone size.** Draw symbols with `K.icon(name, {x, y, size, fill}, parent)`: `share` (a curved arrow pointing right), `play`, `bell`, `like`, `comment` and `next` (an arrow pointing right). They are filled shapes on a 24-unit grid, drawn for this kit, and each is marked `data-icon`. An improvised mark doesn't read at phone size: a lone chevron used as a share icon was unreadable on an end screen (2026-11-03). `check` flags any `data-icon` whose drawn symbol, at its largest while fully shown, is under 18 px on its longer side with the frame shown 360 px wide (`--icon-min`; about 96 px on a 1920 frame). It also saves the end screen (the group a kit marks `data-endscreen`, in the middle of its last fully shown stretch) 360 px wide as `review/endscreen-360.png`, for a look at the size a phone shows it. Trade-offs: the size floor can't tell a clear symbol from a muddled one, which is what the 360 px look is for. An icon a kit draws without `data-icon` isn't measured. The test samples every 0.5 s and adds about 3 s to `check` on a one-minute film.
**The channel logo keeps its colours.** A kit marks every drawing of the channel logo (ident, watermark, chapter card, end screen) with `data-logo="<where>"` on its group, and the film names its channel with `"channel"` in script.json. The palette lives outside the repo in `~/.motion-studio/brand/<channel>.json` (`MS_BRAND_DIR` overrides the folder), as `{"palette": {"ink": "#111111", "accent": "#3366cc", "paper": "#fafafa"}, "delta_e": 3}`. `check` (a flag; `--no-brand` skips it) and `final` (a failure) shoot each stretch where a marked logo is fully shown, once as drawn and once with the logo hidden, to find the logo's own pixels. Each interior pixel, 2 px in from every edge, is measured against the nearest palette colour by CIEDE2000, and a logo fails when more than 2% of them are over `delta_e`. Style packs must never recolour a logo: draw it in the palette colours, above any style filter, grain and the vignette. A logo under the engine's vignette in a corner measured 42% off. This came from the owner catching a recoloured logo in a pixel-style episode (2026-11-03). Trade-offs: it measures what a viewer sees, so a grade the logo sits under counts against it even when the logo is drawn right. It samples every 0.5 s, so a logo up for less than that can be missed. A logo with no `data-logo` mark isn't checked. A logo meant to fade, tint or animate in fails only while fully shown.
**Transitions breathe.** A segment's `"join"` says how it follows the one before: `"transition"` (a new place, subject or chapter) or `"continue"`. Unset, a segment that starts a new chapter is a transition and every other is a continuation. `timeline` holds the segment before a transition longer, on its last picture and in the voice gap, so the gap into the transition is the film's usual continuation gap (the median, from the aligned words) plus 0.3 s (`--transition-hold`; 0 turns it off). Continuations stay tight. Each timeline segment records its `join` and `hold_after`, and a Short takes its holds from its long-form film so its sound cut still lines up. `check` flags a transition whose voice gap is under the usual gap plus the hold (critic.md, joins). This came from the owner's note on 2026-11-03. Trade-offs: re-running `timeline` on an older film lengthens it by up to about 0.3 s at each chapter start. Where clips carry more silence than usual, a transition can already be long enough and gets no hold. Without word times, the hold is a flat 0.3 s.

**Paid voices wait for the final animation pass** (owner's rule, 2026-11-24). Drafts, animatics and every review cut are voiced with a free engine whose weights are licensed for commercial use: Kokoro (Apache-2.0) by default, or an open model run on Colab through `run_colab.sh` when one reads better than Kokoro locally. Candidates being compared on 2026-11-24: Chatterbox (MIT) and Dia-1.6B (Apache-2.0). Not allowed, because their weights forbid commercial use: F5-TTS (CC-BY-NC), OpenAudio S1 (CC-BY-NC-SA) and XTTS (Coqui Public Model Licence). A paid voice (ElevenLabs) is used once, for the final read, after the owner signs off the final animation pass; that saves paid characters on lines that would change. The sign-off goes in script.json as `"animation_signed_off": {"by": "<name>", "on": "<date>"}`, written only on the owner's word. Until it is there, `lines` won't hand over lines for a paid voice, `voiced` won't record a paid clip, and `timeline` flags any. Trade-off: the draft voice and the final one differ in pace and phrasing, so word cues and holds move when the final read lands; run `align`, `timeline` and `check` again on the final voice before the render.

**One voice per film.** `"voice"` in script.json (default: Kokoro `am_michael`, model pack v1.0, speed 1.0, the owner's pick on 2026-10-23) applies to every clip; a drama sets `"voices": {"<speaker>": {...}}`. A human read is `{"engine": "human"}`. Voice each clip with exactly those settings, then run `voiced` (`voiced <film> s05 --speed 1.17` records an exception honestly, and `timeline` then names it). `check` also flags any clip spoken more than 15% faster or slower than the film's median pace (spoken letters per second, from the word times), which catches a speed slip even when nothing was recorded; a 1.17 re-voice showed as +37% on the test film. It also flags a passage: three or more clips in a row that are each at least 5% off the median in the same direction (toll-text's opening ran 6 to 11% fast across its first scenes and sounded rushed, under the single-clip limit, 2026-10-24). Re-voice those clips or loosen the read. A Short copies its clips' records. Trade-offs: the record trusts whoever runs `voiced` about the settings (only the words and the file are checked), so the pace check is the backstop; it cannot tell two voices of the same pace apart, and a film voiced before this needs one `voiced` run before `timeline` will go.

**What the voice says.** The narration is written for the screen and the captions. When the voice must be given something else, `"say"` maps a written phrase to its spoken form, film-wide at the top of script.json and per segment (`"say": {"John Does": "John Doughs", "ezpay-toll.example": "ezpay toll dot example"}`). `lines` hands the TTS the spoken form; captions and word cues keep the written words, and `align` times the spoken words back onto them (word for word when the counts match, else all on the phrase's first word). `lines` and `check` lint the spoken text for: known misreads (`say.KNOWN`; Kokoro reads the surname plural "Does" as the verb, toll-text 2026-10-23), web addresses, phone-style numbers, long digit strings without commas, letter-digit codes, and times or dates in figures. Amounts, short numbers, years and ordinals pass. Add a `KNOWN` entry whenever a listen-through catches a new misread. Misreads with a fix that holds in every sentence go in `say.DEFAULT_SAY` instead, which every film gets under its own map (a film entry for the same phrase wins): it splits "twenty-eight" through "ninety-eight" into two words, because Kokoro runs the hyphenated form together (toll-text, 2026-10-24). A film voiced before a default was added stops at `timeline` with "voiced for other words" on the clips it changes; re-voice those clips. Words spelt one way and said two ways are `say.HOMOGRAPHS`, in two tiers.
- **Block tier.** These have both readings in the same slot in narration, so the voice's part-of-speech guess is a coin toss: live, lives, read, lead(s), wind(s), tear(s), wound, bow(s), row(s), bass. `lines` and `voiced` refuse a line containing one until it is rewritten or pinned. To pin a reading, give the phrase misaki's inline phonemes in the say map, for example `"say": {"barely live": "barely [live](/lˈIv/)"}`. In misaki's alphabet, A is eɪ, I is aɪ, O is oʊ, W is aʊ and Y is ɔɪ. Captions keep the plain word, and alignment strips the markup. The owner's rule is to avoid "live" in any form (2026-10-24), so prefer the rewrite.
- **Warn tier.** These are noun/verb stress pairs and similar words, where the part of speech usually settles the reading: close, record(s), present, object, minute, use, content, invalid, desert, refuse, produce. `lines` lists them as LISTEN and `check` reports them for the listen-through without failing.
- **Not listed.** "Living" has one pronunciation.

`--allow-homographs` hands a line over anyway, for one that a listen-through has confirmed reads right. Trade-offs: refusing a common word like "use" would stop most films, which is why it is only a warning. A pinned word is read exactly as written, so a wrong phoneme string is read wrong every time, and pinning only works through a voice runner that parses misaki's markup. Trade-off: the lint is pattern-based and never hears the audio, so it misses misreads it has no rule for, and a name like "Mary Does" in a title-case line can be flagged when it is the verb.

**Word cues.** Any object inside `data` with `"at": "<word or phrase>"` gets an absolute time `t` when `timeline` runs (`"nth": 2` for a repeat, `"offset": -0.1` to lead). Things that must be read land 0.05 s before their word. `timeline` prints a PROBLEM line for any cue word that isn't in the narration. List items, flow nodes, hub spokes, compare items and statement lines accept `{"text": …, "at": …}` in place of a plain string; `stat` and `sms` take `"at"` on their data to start the count or the message on a word, and `stat` takes `"notes": [{"text", "at"}]`, follow-up lines under the label, so a long stat scene doesn't sit still after the count. A stat scene also rises slowly from the scene's start (not the count's: a number cued to a late word used to leave the scene still until that word, and an 18 s stat cued 15 s in measured 0.037 whatever its drift) (`"stat_drift": true` in engine.json, which `init` writes for new films): a pan of 14 px a second at 1080 lines, easing in over 0.6 s and slowing so the scene never rises more than 12% of the frame's height; a number sets the speed in px a second, `false` holds still, and a segment's own `"drift"` overrides it. A pan, not a push, because text rescaled every frame shimmers. How much motion a pan makes depends on how much is drawn: at 10 px a second a short "$4.3M" with a one-line label measured 0.22 in final's motion units and a long label 0.34, against final's 0.2 floor, and with no drift the hold measured 0.006. 14 px a second keeps the sparsest stat about 50% over the floor. Why: a 7.5 s stat held still for 5 s after its count and failed final (2026-11-25). `check` renders every stat scene of 2 s or more and flags one that will fail. The count-up's digit steps are exempt from final's pop check: as the count eases out, the number holds for a few frames and then steps, which read as pops; final works out the exact frames where the number changes and allows only those. Trade-offs: an older film's stat scenes hold still until its engine.json says `"stat_drift": true`, and both the engine update (new page code) and turning it on (a film-wide setting) make `render --only` refuse, so the film renders whole once. The drift keeps its speed (14 px/s, or `drift` on the segment or in its data) for the whole hold: it rises to the top margin, kept under the HUD cards while they are up, then carries on diagonally into the side room. A film whose own `film.html` is older than the drift never moves its stats, whatever the settings. `check` now tests the page and says so, rather than telling you to raise `drift`; `longform.py init` refreshes the engine files. A stat over about 15 s drifts slower than 10 px a second (the rise cap) and a sparse one can fall under the floor; check flags it, and the fix is notes or a shorter scene. A flagged stat now says how far the page actually moved it (px and px a second at 1080 lines): a drift at full speed whose frames still measure low means the ink is too sparse or too pale against the ground to change many pixels, so raise `drift`, add notes, or darken the text colours.

## Scene types

Text-led (use sparingly; `check` flags a film that is more than 40% these): `title`, `chapter`, `statement`, `quote`, `list`.
Diagrams: `compare`, `stat`, `bars`, `chart`, `flow`, `hub`, `sms` (a phone with a message), `cutaway` (a to-scale section of a real object).

- **`chart`**: a line chart on a paper card, for paths that diverge (growth under different conditions: density, competition, startup money, ads). `data.series` is `[{label, points: [[x, y], ...], color?, at?}]`; each line draws on left to right over `draw` seconds (default 1.6) from its cue word `at` (or staggered across the scene), so it appears as the narrator names its factor, and its label lands at its end on a plate. Labels that would collide are pushed apart and joined to their line's end by a leader. `x` is `{label?, ticks?: [x, ...] or [[x, "Yr 1"], ...], min?, max?}`. `y` is `{label?, format?: "money" | "number" | "percent", ticks?, min?, max?}`; on a money axis the axis and its labels are red below zero and green above, like the HUD cash card. `zero_line: true` draws a dashed line at 0 and keeps it in view. `note` is a small attribution bottom right ("our model, illustrative"): say so whenever the numbers are a model, not data. `title` is optional. The card is the film's own paper when that is light, else warm cream, with near-black ink (`paper` and `ink` override); the default line colours are ink, the film's accent, then blue, violet, brown and teal. The camera pushes slowly in on the frame's centre (1.0 to 1.04 across the scene) so a held chart keeps moving, and the layout leaves room for that push. Every label is at reading size, so keep line labels to two or three words; a long label narrows the plot. On a 9:16 Short the plot is narrow, so use at most three lines there. The source line sits on a plate on a chart, as on panels and maps.
  - **Marks** (`data.marks: [{"series": label or index, "x", "text", "at"?}]`): a dot on that line at x with a plate beside it ("paid back ~2 yrs"). It pops in once the line has drawn past x, or on its cue word if that comes later. Each plate takes the spot near its dot that covers the least of any line. It keeps clear of the other plates, the end labels, the tick labels and the axes, and gets a leader when it sits away from its dot. A mark with no clear spot warns, and `check` flags it.
  - **Held chart** (`"drawn": true`): the chart starts with every line and mark already drawn, so it can hold across two narration beats without blinking. A drawn chart that follows a chart doesn't wipe in, and a run of charts joined by `drawn` shares one camera push, so the zoom doesn't jump back at the join. Give it the same series and axes as the chart before it, or the change shows as a cut. Add a mark with its own `at` to make it pop in on the second beat.
  - A `"counter": null` on two segments in a row no longer breaks the page (fixed 2026-11-27).

- **`compare`**: two columns, `left` and `right`, each `{title, icon?, items}`. Both headings (and icons: `phone`, `bank`, `card`, `person`, `money`, `web`, `doc`, `x`, `check`, in a ring above the heading) are up in the first second, and items land row by row, left then right, across the first 70% of the segment (or on their `at` words), so both sides stay on screen while the viewer compares. Long items wrap; a row is as tall as its taller side. `accent: "left"` moves the highlight off the right column. `strike: true` crosses out each left item as its right item lands, for "instead of" pairs; it is off by default because striking the real side of a real-versus-fake pair reads as wrong (it was always on before 2026-10-23). A side's `color` (`"red"`, `"green"` or a hex) tints its ring, icon and heading, and `fill: true` makes the ring a solid disc with the icon in white. Red and green come from the palette's `RED` and `GREEN` when it has them, otherwise #e0483e and #3fae5a, both of which read on dark and light grounds. For a do/don't pair the owner wants a bold green tick and a red cross (2026-10-23): `{"icon": "check", "color": "green", "fill": true}` and `{"icon": "x", "color": "red", "fill": true}`. Trade-off: red and green alone fail for red-green colour-blind viewers, so the tick and cross shapes carry the meaning and the colour only reinforces it. In a Short the columns stack.
Closing beats can show our own free research tool: real screenshots of its answers for the episode's business, captured as `capture` scenes, work better than drawn stand-ins (Survive Year 1, 2026-11-27). The panel `screen` overlay is the way to show numbers on a device inside a still.

Evidence: `capture`, a captured screenshot of a real page or message (below, under real things on screen). `data.real` names the capture. `frame` is `browser` (the default, with the page's address in the bar), `phone` or `none`. `scroll: [from, to]` moves down a tall capture over the segment (0 to 1 of what doesn't fit). `marks: [{box: [x, y, w, h], at, label}]` draws accent boxes on the image (0 to 1 of the image) on their words, and the camera leans toward the newest one. The footer shows the segment's `source`, or else the capture's `credit`, or else the site's domain.
People and evidence (their own set; they wipe on and off left to right instead of cutting). The wipe's edge is laid out on the frame, not in the scene, so a scene whose camera has panned or zoomed still fills the frame to its edges; the stage set's wall and floor run half a frame past each side, more than the camera's widest lean (a figure at x 0.12 used to leave a 55 px unpainted strip at the left edge, Longtok 2026-10-25):

- **`stage`**: rigged figures on a set (`room`, `street`, `desk`, `blank`). Cast entries take figure options (below) plus `x` (0–1), `h` (px, default 540), `pose`, `enter: "left"|"right"` (walks in). Beats: `pose`, `face`, `emote` (`? ! $ … x`), `say` / `think` (bubble; `""` clears it with a 0.3 s fade), `notify` (a message card beside the phone; `from` changes its "MESSAGES · now" header, e.g. for a browser or an app), `sms` (the text message itself coming off the phone: `sender`, default "+1 (555) 010-0199", and `senderNote`, default "Text Message", above the body in a grey bubble, laid out like the `sms` scene; it is placed like `notify` and keeps clear of a notification already up), `glow` (the phone lights and buzzes), `move` (walk to a new x). The camera leans toward whoever acted last, panning by fractions of a pixel (`g.__cam.frac`; on whole pixels a slow ease stepped the set every other frame). Nothing on a stage changes in a single frame, because final's pop check fails that. Pose changes anticipate when the film's `anticipate` is on: a lean back before a big pose change, an overshoot that settles, and an optional `hold` (frames) on a pose beat (the `realise` pose holds 2). `init` writes `engine.json` with `"anticipate": true`, so films made from 2026-10-25 on get it. A film with no `engine.json` (any film made before) keeps the old moves, so its re-renders don't change, and `"anticipate"` in script.json overrides either way. A Short copies its film's `engine.json`. Bubbles and cards hang from the plain move, so their text moves no more than before. Trade-off: the lean back starts 0.12 s before the word, so a pose beat's first motion now comes slightly early. A bubble that replaces another waits while the old one fades for 0.22 s, so it lands 0.22 s after its cue. A change of facing, whether from a `face` beat or from setting off on or finishing a walk, turns over 0.2 s, and the bubbles slide across with it. A notification or `sms` is placed clear of every bubble that will be up while it shows, including ones still to come, so it never jumps when one appears or goes. A card keeps off every head (weight 4) and leans on its holder's torso less readily than on the holder's legs. Words under a card are lost, so the kicker counts as heavily as a bubble (weight 3), and on a Short no card rises above the safe area's top. A card tries its usual wrap, a narrower one (up to 6 lines, for a spot beside a figure near the frame edge) and a wider one (for a spot overhead), and takes a reflow only when it covers clearly less; a reflow never drops words. When a scene has no clear spot, for example a long message in an upright two-shot, check fails it as "card over <id>'s head" or "card over <id>'s body" (a quarter of anyone's torso). Shorten the message, move the cast, or use the `sms` scene instead (Longtok found a card covering its holder from the head down, then one over its holder's torso and legs, 2026-10-24). Trade-offs: a card may now change shape between scenes, and keeping cards clear of the kicker and the Short's top edge leaves fewer spots, so a few more scenes fail check. These come from the Trapdoor kit's stage_smooth (2026-10-24). `kicker` is a fixed label top left. `light: "left"|"right"` lights the set from that side: a warm pool on that side, a falloff to dark on the far side (over the figures too, under bubbles and cards), and a soft shadow on the floor under every prop and figure, cast away from the light. `foreground: true` puts dark, out-of-focus shapes on the frame's bottom corners, in front of the action and below knee height (an armchair and a tall plant in a room, a chair back and a desk lamp at a desk, a railing and a bollard on the street, a low ledge on blank). Every piece stops at about knee height, and each corner's pieces slide outward, off the frame if they must, to stay clear of every figure's box widened by 12% each side, wherever that figure stands or walks to in the scene. They also keep clear of the scene's source line (bottom left on a wide film) wherever the camera goes in the scene, plus a pad that takes in a soft piece's blur: a corner piece slides outward, usually off the frame, and the blank set's ledge stops above the line. `check` fails any text drawn straight onto a foreground piece ("on the stage foreground"), because the piece's edges run through the words; posts and furniture through the source line made it hard to read on a phone (Longtok, 2026-10-25). Trade-off: a scene with a source loses its left foreground piece on a wide film. They sit on the corners where the camera rests on average and slide 35% further than the set when it pans, so the scene reads as having depth. Both are off unless set, so older films render the same. `look: "flat"` draws both for a flat, outlined art style such as a marker-on-paper kit: each shadow is a hard-edged shape in one darker tone of the floor (`shadow: "#hex"` sets it, for a kit that paints its own floor) at 25% opacity, offset away from the light by 6% of the object's height, with no blur and no light pool or falloff; the foreground pieces are solid shapes in that tone with an outline in `ink: "#hex"` (default the palette's `INK`), unblurred, running off the frame's bottom edge (Longtok, 2026-10-25). On the engine's own dark sets a flat shadow barely shows, because a darker tone of a near-black floor is nearly the same colour; the flat look is for light floors. The foreground covers the feet of a figure standing at the frame's edge. The lamp drawn in a room stands on the right whatever `light` says. The shapes are fixed per set and not yet configurable. Each set is dressed so the place reads at a glance: `room` has a window with a moon, a framed picture, a sofa with a cushion, a lamp and a plant; `desk` has a monitor on a stand, keyboard, mug, papers, a drawer unit and a plant; `street` has buildings with lit windows and a street lamp. `screen: {url, title?, lines?}` puts a browser page on the desk's monitor. The monitor is drawn at a monitor's size beside a person, so its text is set dressing and too small to read; say what matters in a `notify` or bubble too (notifications keep clear of the screen). - **Panel callouts** (a `panel` with `callouts`): facts animated into a still's dead space.     - `stat`: a big value (red below zero, green above) over a one- or two-line label.
    - `note`: a quote or short line in italics, up to three lines.
    - `source`: a small SOURCE kicker over the source's name, with `value` as its date.

A faint feathered wash only evens the grain. The words write on left to right like a pen, a value gets a hand-drawn accent underline, and a bar is an ink outline filled with accent gouache.

    - `"card"` is the old paper card in the HUD's style, which rises in.
    - `"tag"` is the default in a collage film (below): a scrap of the map's paper with tape, slapped on at its word.

The page tests every place at the framed crop across the card's whole time on screen. It picks the highest quiet place nearest the subject, so cards stack down a column beside what they're about. When nothing is quiet enough the card goes to the least busy place and a warning says so; `check` flags every page warning. The warning needs a real share of the card on drawing (more than 2 cells, or 6% of its footprint): a short callout whose edge touches a line or two no longer flags (a two-word callout flagged on 2 cells, 2026-11-27). After a look at full size, `"busy_ok": true` on a callout silences its warning.

    - "Busy" is measured, not understood. A flat sky and a flat wall both count as quiet, and a plain patch can belong to the subject (a plain shirt).

- **Panel fx** (a `panel` with `fx`): motion drawn over one locked still, so the drawing itself never moves: `"fx": [{"type": "rain"}, {"type": "beam", "from": [0.48, 0.55], "aim": [0.7, 0.75], "at": "flashlight"}, {"type": "flicker", "pos": [0.64, 0.27]}, {"type": "steam", "pos": [0.31, 0.6]}]`. Types: `rain` (`density` 0.5, `angle` 12°, `speed`, `length`, `area` [x, y, w, h]), `snow` (`density`, `speed`, `size`, `area`), `beam` (a light cone from `from` toward `aim`, `spread` 16°, sweeping `sweep` 6° either side over `period` 4 s, `reach` 0.6 of the width), `flicker` (a glow at `pos`, `radius` 0.12, `amount` 0.5, `rate` 6 Hz; with no `pos` the whole frame dims a little) `steam` (puffs rising from `pos`, `rise` 0.25, `width` 0.06, `puffs` 6, `period` 3 s) and `highlight` (a ring in the film's accent that springs open on its cue around `pos`, radius `r` 0.15 of the width, at most 0.25; the rest of the still dims by `dim` 0.28, at most 0.4, and `zoom` above 1 magnifies inside like a lens; it eases in once and holds, so it never flashes). Every effect takes `at` (starts on that word, fading in over 0.3 s), `hold` (seconds it lasts), `color` and `opacity`. Positions are 0..1 of the still, so an effect sits on what it belongs to and moves with the panel's camera. Everything is a pure function of time and seeded, so renders repeat exactly. A flicker is smooth noise, and its glow is capped at radius 0.2 and amount 0.6, and the whole-frame dim at 0.08, so it can't fail the flash-rate check. An unknown type is a LINT line. This is the route for weather, light and steam on a cold open: Codex edits between flipbook frames moved the figure and redrew the texture (likeness 0.71 and 0.82 against the base on the first test, 2026-11-25), which flips as jitter, so flipbook edits suit only a deliberate cut to a new drawing. - **Clip panels** (a `panel` with `clip`): a video clip (an mp4 in the film, such as a Wan image-to-video take) plays inside the panel, frame-exact: `"clip": "clips/c1_s7.mp4"`, with `image` as the poster (no `image`: the clip's first frame is the poster). `timeline` extracts the clip once into `clips/frames/<name>-<hash>/` as numbered JPEGs, cut to cover the panel with Lanczos (Wan's 832x480 is stretched about 2.3x to 1920x1080, so it reads softer than a 1920 still), and writes `data.clip_sched`, one entry per video frame. Pans and tilts travel 5% of the still either side of centre at a zoom of at least 1.12, which leaves room for the whole move; at the old 1.08 the camera sat against the still's edge for the first and last 40% of the shot (changed 2026-11-25, so re-rendered WIDE and OTS shots are a little tighter and move for their whole length). `check` predicts each panel's motion before any render, from its still and its camera path, measured the way `final`'s motion check measures the video, and flags a panel whose quietest 2 s falls under the motion floor or within 20% of it. The floor comes from the last render when there is one (final's rule needs the whole film's median), else from the panels; either way it is never above 0.2 (review.md). The prediction leaves out fades, caption cards and grain, so it reads low (about half the measured value on a test close-up): a panel flagged as "barely above" usually passes, and one flagged "too little" fails. Fix with `"camera": "slow-push"` (a centred push from 1.0 to 1.14, the owner's preference), a pan, or a shorter shot. Don't fix it with a strong or off-centre push: past z 1.3 it reads as "zooming at nothing" (owner's review, 2026-11-26), and `check` flags any panel camera that zooms past 1.3. A quiet flag on a slow push is acceptable. `--no-motion-estimate` skips it. `flashback: true` grades sepia with a heavy vignette. `caption_card` (`"Three days earlier"` or `{text, at, hold}`) is a boxed caption top left; while the HUD's tag card is up at any moment of its hold, it stacks under the tag instead of over it (they collided, 2026-11-26). `freeze` (`true` or `{at}`) stops the camera and drains the colour (a cliffhanger freeze). `shake: {at, amp}` is a decaying jolt on an impact. Panels cut by default; `enter`/`exit: "fade"` fades through black. Several beats may reuse one image with a different `shot`, `focus` or `camera`. Write the generator's `prompt` and `char_in_frame` on the panel's first beat; `longform.py panels <film>` lists each unique image once with its prompt, characters and whether it exists (`out/panels.json`, for the image runner). Panels come from the image model at 1344x768. `longform.py upscale <film>` (every panel still the script names, plus each one's take siblings `<shot>_s<seed>.png`, so whichever take the owner picks is ready) runs Real-ESRGAN (`realesrgan-ncnn-vulkan`, BSD-3, anime model) at 4x on each one, then Lanczos down to cover 2304x1296 and centre-crops, writing to `panels-hd/` at the same relative path. 2304x1296 is the frame times the deepest camera push (ECU, 1.2), so a push-in still shows one image pixel per screen pixel. `timeline` uses the upscaled copy when one is newer than its source, and lists any panel under 1920 wide that has none. On the K-factory test (2026-10-17) this took 7.5 s a panel, against 1,541 s for a native 1920x1088 render, and gave sharper lines. A drama segment also takes `speaker` (whose voice reads the line; `lines --json` lists them for TTS), `mono: true` (inner monologue: italic in the caption track, and warm italic if captions are burned in) and `sfx` (foley by kind on a cue word: `["paper slap", {"kind": "low hit", "at": "envelope"}]`; a kind with no recorded library clip stays silent). `bed` (`"rain glass"` or a list) loops an ambience clip under the whole segment, about 10 dB under the hits; back-to-back segments with the same bed share one continuous run, which fades in and out over 0.8 s at its edges. Kind names are one role however they are spelled: "paper slap", "Paper_Slap" and "paper-slap" all match the library role `paper-slap`. Hits are cut to the time their kind may ring (`SFX_MAX`: a gavel 3 s, a slap 0.8 s, unknown kinds 1.5 s); beds are never cut.

**Panel quality** (from K-factory's anatomy research, 2026-10-17). Small step-distilled image models break hands, limbs and busy scenes. The best licence-safe vision judge agreed with people only about 60% of the time, and neither ChatGPT nor mflux 0.23 takes a mask. So the engine lints prompts before generation, a person marks faults on a sheet after it, and each fault is repaired by redrawing a crop (with ChatGPT; local models are drafts only).

- `longform.py lint <film>` (or a JSON list of panel records with `prompt`, `shot`, `refs`, `plate`) warns on: more than 2 reference images (`data.refs` plus `data.plate`); more than 80 words of scene description (sentences like "Image 1 is the identity reference for …", a last sentence shared by at least half the prompts, and any sentence matching `--boiler REGEX` don't count); more than 2 characters in frame; more than 2 visible hands; contact between two people's hands or arms; "while", "then", "as he" or "idle" (maybe more than one action per character); a plate on a CU, ECU or INS shot; and negative phrasing, which klein ignores because it has no negative prompt ("no text" is left alone). Hand and action counts are heuristics: hands hidden in a pocket, a lap, behind the back or out of frame are not counted, and repeated mentions of one person's hand count once, but a pronoun and a name for the same person count twice. `panels` prints how many prompts have warnings.
- `longform.py review <film> [--checks checks.json]` writes `out/review/index.html`, with every panel numbered in film order. Open it from the film folder. Click a panel to mark it, drag on it to box a region, and type what is wrong in its note. The page keeps the marks in that browser, downloads them as `marks.json`, and prints a `repair` command for each box, with the note as its prompt. `--checks` is any checker's output, `{image: {"verdict": "FAIL", "reason", "boxes": [[x, y, w, h] in 0-1]}}`, drawn as red outlines. A checker only orders the queue; the person decides.
- `longform.py repair <film> --beat b07 --box x,y,w,h --prompt "Redraw the right hand in image 1: thumb on top, four fingers wrapped around the glass, five fingers; keep the cuff, lineart and colours unchanged"`. The box is in panel pixels, or 0-1 as the sheet gives it. The command crops a square 2.25 times the box's longer side (at least 640 px), moved to stay inside the panel, and redraws it at its own size (a crop over 1024 is scaled down to 1024). A resize there and back halves the fine detail: a Lanczos round trip alone measured 0.48 of the panel's detail, which is most of the softness K-factory saw in klein repairs. `--gen-size` forces a size, and a crop enlarged that way goes through Real-ESRGAN first when it is installed (`$REALESRGAN_BIN`, `"realesrgan"` in `~/.motion-studio.json`, or PATH). The default route is ChatGPT images through codex (`--model chatgpt`): the crop goes to it as the edit base with the note as the change, and it is asked for the same square back. It needs `--allow-codex` (or `CODEX_STILLS_OK=1`), because each take is one image from the Codex allowance; a take that comes back non-square is asked again once. `--faceless` adds the no-face rule. Local models are drafts only (picture-truth.md rule 15): `--draft-local` runs Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 at 6-bit (Apache-2.0, about 3 to 6 minutes a seed), or klein 4B with `--model klein` (about 45 to 75 s a seed), and the render refuses a panel while such a repair is accepted. `--ref` adds a second image, such as the sleeve and hand cropped from the character sheet. One run makes three takes (seeds, on the local route) by default (`--seeds`).
- **The default method is `pair`** (picture-truth.md rule 16). On the ChatGPT route it runs `markup` and `whole` together, one take each (2 Codex images), as two repairs. Gate both at full size and `accept` the one that fixes the note. Crop is the last resort, and the only method on the local draft route. A redraw softer than 0.85 of the panel's detail on the paste margin is sharpened to match it (an unsharp mask, logged as `sharpen`). `--no-sharpen` turns that off.
- `--method whole` (ChatGPT only) sends the whole panel with "change only ...". The answer is mapped back onto the panel the same way as markup's, and the paste gets a wider blend by default (64 px margin, 48 px feather), because a whole-panel answer is redrawn everywhere and a 16 px blend left a seam. It won the first A/B (picture-truth.md rule 16).
- `--method markup` (ChatGPT only) sends the whole panel instead of the crop: the clean panel as image 1 and a copy with the paste region outlined in red as image 2. The answer is mapped back onto the panel (resized, or the better aligned of a stretch and a centre crop when it comes back at another shape), the same crop is cut from it, and everything below applies unchanged. The log records how much the model redrew outside the outline and any marker red left in. It suits a fault where a crop would cut through a neighbouring object (a tilted unit). To pick a method by evidence, run `python3 editab.py <film> --image <panel> --box x,y,w,h --change "..." --allow-codex`: it makes one take each by crop, markup and whole (one Codex image each); the whole take is shown both pasted and as the model returned it. It scores each take by how much it changed outside the box and in it, and writes a blind lettered sheet for a judge that knows the fault but not the methods. The results land in `repairs/ab/<image>/NN/report.md`.
- The paste covers the box plus `--margin` (32 px), so a redraw that spills a little past the box is kept. It blends over `--feather` (16 px) inside that edge, rising from 0 at the edge with a smoothstep, so nothing outside the paste changes and the box itself is fully replaced. Sides on the panel border get no blend. Before pasting, the redraw's colours are matched to the original on a 20 px band around the paste (per-channel gain and offset, fitted twice so new content in the band does not skew it, gain held to 0.8-1.25); `--no-color-match` turns it off. Each seed is checked and the checks are logged in `repairs/log.json`. A seed that moved less than 10% of the box by more than 60 levels is "no edit" and is not offered (`--min-change`, 0 keeps all); its raw redraw is still in `compare_<seed>.png`. A seed whose redraw differs from the panel on more than 3% of a 12 px band just outside the paste is flagged: the paste cuts that content off, so grow the box or the margin. A seed whose fine detail on the margin is under 0.7 or over 1.4 of the panel's is flagged as softer or sharper. Before generating, the command lists the strong lines in the panel that cross each edge of the paste.
- Placing the box: cover the whole limb or object with its reflection and shadow, because whatever continues outside the paste stays as drawn and breaks where the redraw moves it. Keep box edges off faces, and put them across flat areas (background, a plain sleeve) rather than through eyes, mouths or line-dense areas. The engine has no face detector to enforce this (the common licence-safe ones miss drawn faces), so the crossing list is the only automatic hint.
- Candidates and before/after crops land in `repairs/<image path>/rNN/`, and the run is logged with its beats, box, paste region, prompt, model, seeds and checks in `repairs/log.json`. `--gen-args` passes anything else to mflux on the draft route (a Lightning LoRA, `--steps`). The mflux binaries are found through `"mflux": {"bin": "<venv>/bin"}` in `~/.motion-studio.json` (or `$MFLUX_BIN`), else on PATH. `"mflux": {"klein": ["--model", "<local 8-bit folder>", "--base-model", "flux2-klein-4b"]}` (and `"qwen": [...]`) replaces a model's default arguments; without it, the built-in names make mflux download the full official weights (about 15 GB for klein). `--image` works on a bare folder of panels with no `script.json`. The panel is untouched until `longform.py accept <film> panels/p07/r02 [--seed 2]`, which pastes that region onto the panel as it is now, with the colour match logged for that seed, so two repairs of one panel both survive. Repairs logged before this paste are accepted with the old one (feather outside the box). `revert` restores the panel from before a repair, newest first. After either, run `upscale` again; `timeline` flags the stale copy.
- Trade-offs: the model redraws the whole crop and only the paste is kept, so a seam can still show where line weight or shading differs (look at `compare_<seed>.png`). The wider crop for a small box gives the model more context, but the box is a smaller part of what it sees, so a small fix may need a more specific prompt. The 10% rule drops a real but small fix inside a large box; use a tighter box or `--min-change 0`. The colour match assumes the shift is the same across the crop; a redraw that relights only the box is not corrected. The checks are pixel statistics, not judgements of the drawing. A .jpg panel loses a little quality on each accept. Not used, for licence reasons: ADetailer and ultralytics (AGPL), MeshGraphormer and HandRefiner (they need MANO, which is non-commercial), OpenPose (non-commercial weights, which also rules out mflux's `--control pose:`).

## Real things on screen

A scene that shows a real thing (a scam text, a company's site, a headline, an agency page) must show a capture of it. It must never show a convincing drawing of one. Captures are listed in `sources/captures.json`:

```json
{"ftc-toll-alert": {"file": "sources/ftc-toll-alert.png", "url": "https://consumer.ftc.gov/…", "captured": "2026-10-19",
                    "licence": "Public domain (US federal government work)", "credit": "FTC.gov"}}
```

`file`, `url`, `captured` (YYYY-MM-DD) and `licence` are required. For a page that isn't public domain, `licence` holds the fair-use note: commentary or news reporting, cropped to the part being discussed, credited. `credit` is optional. Show a capture with the `capture` scene, or point a drawn element at one with `"real": "<id>"`. Elements that look real are an `sms` scene, a stage beat with `notify` or `sms`, and a board card with a `web`, `doc`, `phone`, `bank` or `card` icon (a `kind: "note"` card is exempt). Each of these needs a `real` id or `"illustrative": true`. `illustrative` can go on the element, on the scene's `data`, or on the segment, and it is for things that are clearly made up, such as an example text written for the film. `timeline` and `render` stop and list every element that has neither marker, or whose capture is unlisted, missing a field or missing its file. There is no switch to skip the check.

This supports the Longtok rule: complaints are attributed to the agency or outlet that published them, so the capture is the attribution. Never name a company as a scam.

Trade-offs: every real-looking element needs either a capture or an explicit "made up" mark, so older scripts stop at `timeline` until they are marked. Most sites can't be reached from the cloud, so captures are taken on the Mac (a browser screenshot saved to `sources/`). A `real` id on a drawn `sms` records where the message came from, but the drawing is still a recreation; use `capture` to show the message itself.

## Collage style

`"style": "collage"` in script.json draws a film's overlays in the map's collage look (house.js, shared with mapscene.js), so stills, HUD, charts and maps read as one hand. Owner, 2026-11-27: "we're already utilizing the vox effects, so why not utilize them over the stills and etc too?" Off by default; `timeline` fetches the fonts into `fonts/` (mapart.py's set: Libre Caslon, Special Elite, Permanent Marker, Anton; OFL and Apache-2.0, licences beside them).

- Panel callouts default to `"tag"`. The value is in Caslon with a yellow highlighter stroke behind it, and the words are typed in Special Elite capitals (a `note` is in marker). The tag slaps on at its word (1.1, 0.98, 1 on twos, never a fade), boils every 8 frames, and steps off in three steps. `"point"` draws a marker leader with a head, on twos, in ink, or in paper where the picture under it is dark. The paper rides the camera smoothly. Only its own motion steps on twos.
- `data.ink` on a panel: `{"kind": "circle", "place": [x, y], "r": 0.08}`, `{"kind": "arrow", "from": [x, y], "to": [x, y]}`, `{"kind": "x", "place": [x, y]}`, or `{"kind": "paint", "box": [x0, y0, x1, y1]}`. Positions are 0..1 of the still, each item lands on its `at` word and holds to `until` or the panel's end. Ink is the map's tapered marker, drawn on twos then boiling. Paint is a half see-through yellow sweep, left to right over 16 frames. Multiplied, it went olive on grey metal.
- HUD: the cash card is a taped ledger strip (ruled lines, a red margin, Caslon value, Special Elite label), and the change chip is a stub of paper with the amount in its sign's colour. The hero uses the same paper. The tag card is a yellow tag.
- Source line: typed in Special Elite on a torn strip of paper, a touch off level, on every scene.
- Charts: the map's paper, Caslon type, line labels on paper scraps, and the title on a card with a yellow FIG label (`"fig"` changes its text). A chart with no title puts its y label on the card.
- Yellow is palette `HIGHLIGHT`, else the house yellow #EDBD2E. It is not ACCENT, which a warm palette often makes a dark ochre that ink can't be read on. Maps still paint with ACCENT.
- `"paper_grain": true` lays a fibre grain over the paper pieces. Off by default.
- Napkin math (owner's pick, 2026-11-27; works in either style, drawn in the collage look in a collage film):
  - Price tags: a callout with `"kind": "price"` and a `"point"` is a manila hang tag. Its pointed end faces the thing it prices, it is tied to it by a cream twine that pays out with the slap, and it hangs a few degrees askew. Auto placement keeps it near its point and never over it.
  - Running tally: a segment's `"tally": {"title": "Startup", "items": [{"label": "Truck", "value": "$9,500", "at": "truck"}], "total": {"label": "Total", "at": "comes to"}}` puts a taped napkin under the tag card. Each line slaps on at its word. The total draws a marker rule, then counts up to the sum of the lines. Lines carry into later segments' tallies until `"tally": null`, or `"reset": true` starts a new one. More than six lines warns. Callouts keep clear of the napkin, and its numbers count as on screen for the number-at-word and deadspace checks.
  - Ticking cash card: `"cash": true` on a tally item ticks the counter card on that word, down by the item (a cost), or up when the value starts with "+". The item's label captions the change stub. A tick with no counter card up warns.
  - Trade-offs: the napkin takes the top-left corner beside the picture, so stills need room there or the callouts move away. A tick is computed from the card's last value, so a later explicit `"counter"` must agree with the running sum, which `check` does not test.
- Trade-offs: a tag is still a box on the picture, which is what he called "plastered-on"; tape, tilt and a shadow make it read as stuck on, not melded in. Paper covers drawing, so the quiet-room warning still matters. Special Elite's zero looks like a capital O. The map's sound roles have no recordings yet, so the slaps are silent.

## Source line

A segment's `source` shows as "Source: …" bottom left. On panels and maps it sits on a dark plate in light grey, since grey text alone was lost on coastlines and busy stills (owner's review, 2026-11-26); elsewhere it stays plain grey. `"source_plate": true` or `false` on a segment overrides that. Trade-off: the plate is a visible box on every panel and map with a source.

## Beat sync

Owner, 2026-11-27: "improve how well we pace what is on screen/the beats with the voiced lines to make sure it is in sync." `check` reads the timeline (pace.py) and treats each cut and each thing cued to a word as a landing. Cued things include data items with `at`, the counter, the tag, and tally lines and totals.

- It flags any stretch of narration over `--pace-gap` s (4) with nothing new landing. The flag quotes the words said in the gap and suggests a cue word: a number said there first, else the longest content word near the middle. A clip panel is moving footage and is skipped.
- It flags reveals that land at a default time (a callout, ink, a chart line, a bullet) while their own words are said in the segment, and gives the matching word or spoken amount. It reads the same places a cue works on: the reveal lists, a stat's notes, a breakdown's parts, a stat scene's number (matched by its number only, never by its label's words) and a panel's caption card. The item's number is read from its raw text, so "$1,200/mo" is 1,200 and "$15–25" is 15 and 25 (before 2026-11-27 it joined cleaned words, which read the first as 1 and the second as 1,525).
- It flags a number cued to a word said more than 0.7 s before the number itself, naming the cue and the number as said. This one is reported, never changed: a price can fairly land on the thing it prices.
- It reports the rhythm: the median time between landings while the voice runs, and the share of spoken time within 3 s of one. Strong business explainers land something every 2 to 4 s (editing study, 2026-11-27).
- `longform.py pace <film>` writes all of it to review/pace.md. `--apply` writes the matched words into script.json as `at` (the previous copy goes to script.json.bak) and leaves any cue already set alone; with nothing to cue it says so and writes nothing. Run `timeline` after.
- Trade-offs: it reads cues from the data, not the frames, so a scene type's own default-timed reveals don't count as landings until they are cued. Camera moves never count, because a move is not new information. Suggested cue words are a heuristic with no grammar, so read them before using them. Gaps need new content (a tag, ink, a tally line, another still), which only a person or the episode thread can choose.

## Captions

Long-form ships its captions as uploaded caption tracks, not burned in (decided 2026-10-17: the channel adds Spanish and Portuguese dubs as extra audio tracks on the same video, and burned English text would sit under them). `timeline` writes `out/captions.vtt` from the word times, with inner monologue in `<i>` and no speaker labels (no name badge is burned in either). For another language, write the translated lines as `{"<segment id>": "line"}` and run `longform.py captions <film> --lang es --text es.json`: each translation spans its segment's spoken words in the English read, split into pages of at most 42 characters that share the time by length, written to `out/captions.es.vtt`. It stops if a spoken segment has no line. `youtube.py captions <id> out/captions.es.vtt --language es --name Español` attaches a track. `"captions": true` still burns word-lit pages into the picture (about four words a page, spoken words turn from grey to ink, the current word is accent) for a cut that will never carry a dub. Shorts always burn them in (below). Anything else drawn as text in the picture (a panel's `caption_card`, a chart label) stays in English under every dub, so keep it to names, places and dates.

## Shorts

A Short is a 9:16 film re-laid-out from a run of a long-form film's segments. It is never a crop of the 16:9 render. `longform.py short <film> <short> --from s05 --to s09 [--title …]` makes a new film folder. It copies those segments' script entries, voice clips, word times, panel stills and `sources/`, and writes a `script.json` with `"format": "9x16"`, `"captions": true` and `"short_of"` (the long-form film and the segment range). It prints the Short's length and warns when it passes 3 minutes, YouTube's limit for a Short.

Then lay it out in the Short's own `script.json`, which is never overwritten. Put fewer things on screen at once (fewer list items, compare rows, board cards and hub spokes), give it its own camera (panel `focus` and `camera`, board card `x`/`y`, stage cast `x`), and cut text that the narration already says.

- **Layout.** Every scene type has a 9:16 layout. Text is larger and wraps narrower; `compare` stacks its columns with a rule between them; `flow` runs top to bottom with labels to the right; `bars` puts each label above its bar; `hub` becomes an upright oval; and the crowd pulls back until every seat fits; a `stage` holds its camera centred instead of leaning toward whoever acts, because a lean crops figures at the edges of an upright frame. Board card positions (0 to 1) map onto the safe area. The panel still keeps its 16:9 shape, so a 9:16 frame sees about a third of it, and `focus`/`camera` choose which third.
- **Safe area.** YouTube's Shorts controls cover the bottom ~22% and a right rail, so what must be read stays inside x 60–960 and y 220–1220. Captions sit just under that, at y 1260. The source line moves to the top, and there is no chapter label. These numbers are the long-form engine's own; `studio.py`'s 9x16 format still puts captions at y 1560.
- **Sound.** `timeline` checks that every segment lasts exactly as long as it does in the long-form film, then cuts the Short's `out/mix.wav` from the long-form mix: 0.3 s before the first line through the end of the last segment, a 0.4 s fade, then 0.8 s of silence. It also copies the long-form film's `sounds_used.json`, so `credits` works. Run `mix` on the long-form film first. If you changed `--pad` or `--lead` there, use the same values here, or `timeline` prints a PROBLEM. `mix` on a Short refuses unless you pass `--remix`.
- **A film's engine copy.** A film keeps the `film.html` and scripts `init` copied in, so a scene type added to the engine later is unknown to an older film, and its page drew it as the word "undefined" with no page error (two cutaways in a full film, 2026-11-25). `timeline` reports a PROBLEM and `check` stops for any segment whose type the film's `film.html` doesn't define, and both warn when `film.html`, `kit.js`, `figure.js`, `closeup.js`, `mapscene.js`, `three.min.js` or `set3d.js` differ from the engine's copies. `longform.py init <film>` refreshes them and keeps `script.json`; keep a copy first of any engine file edited by hand.
- **Render resumes.** `render` draws the film in 30 s chunks and keeps every finished one in `out/parts`. A chunk that fails because the browser died or timed out (`TargetClosedError`) is retried up to `--retries` times (2) with half the workers. If it still fails, render stops and keeps the rest, and running it again renders only what is missing. Kept chunks are reused only while the page, timeline, assets and settings are unchanged, otherwise render starts over; `--fresh` forces that. Workers are capped by free memory at 1.5 GB each with 2 GB kept free (one worker measured about 1 GB on a plain film), and render says so when it lowers `--workers`. One dead worker at 6 workers cost a full hour before this (2026-10-24).
- **Re-render a few segments.** `render <film> --only s12,s40` (or `--from s12 --to s15`) re-renders just those segments and splices them into the last render, for a fix that touches a few shots (a 12.5-min film is about an hour on the Mac; four segments take a few minutes). Render cuts its parts at every segment's start and 0.6 s after it, so each segment is whole parts, and the next segment's first 0.6 s (where this one's outgoing wipe still draws) is redone with it. A map segment brings its whole run, since the run shares one camera. A change to the map itself (maps/map.json, its tiles or fonts, or `map_drift`) is used only by map segments, so it redoes every map segment instead of stopping the splice, and render names the segments it added. Before 2026-11-25 any map rebuild refused the splice and cost a whole render. The last segment's frames run to the end of the film, so a map that holds through the tail is redone to the last frame. Before splicing, render compares the film with the last render (`out/parts/manifest.json`) and stops, naming what differs, if anything outside the named segments may have changed: the page's code, any segment's timing, another segment, film-wide settings, a named segment's counter, tag or other keys outside its `data` (they carry into later segments), or a changed file the named segments don't use. Then it says to render the whole film. Trade-offs: the first render after this change starts over (the parts are cut differently), and a film now has a part per segment or more, so each render starts more browser pages (a few seconds each, spread over the workers).
- **Page errors stop everything.** A JavaScript error in the film's page is the film's own code, so it fails the same way on every try. `render`, `stills` and every other frame grab stop at the first one and name the error, its file and line, the time and the segment. A render also stops its other chunks and is not retried, and the run exits non-zero. This came after a kit edit put a `//` comment mid-line, which commented out the next statement: every frame of one scene threw, and the render kept retrying that chunk without stopping or reporting (2026-10-26). Before that, page errors were only counted in a warning.
- **The page gate.** `check` first loads the page and seeks to the start, middle and end of every segment, and stops there if it sees any JavaScript error, `console.error` or unhandled promise rejection, listing each distinct one with its time and segment. It takes a second or two. `console.warn` and `console.log` are allowed. `render` runs the same gate before drawing anything. It also refuses to start unless the last `check` passed the gate on this exact version of the film: `review/check-gate.json` records `film_stamp`: the top-level page, scripts, timeline and JSON by content, and every other file by size and time, except what never reaches a frame. That is the `out`, `review`, `logs`, `narration` and `music` folders, dot-files, and files ending `.log`, `.md`, `.txt`, `.tsv`, `.csv`, `.bak`, `.tmp`, `.swp`, `.pid`, `.lock` or `~` (`STAMP_SKIP_DIRS`, `STAMP_SKIP_TYPES`). So a log a kit appends after each step doesn't make the film "changed since check", while an image or script a kit adds in a subfolder still counts. Render's kept chunks use the same rule. `--skip-gate` renders anyway, prints why, and notes it in the status file, but the page gate itself still runs. Other check flags (text size, overlap, hold) don't block a render; they need a person's judgement. Trade-offs: a strict gate blocks a render over a harmless `console.error`, such as a kit logging a missing optional file. Fix the noise or log it with `console.warn`. The gate only seeks three moments per segment, so an error in between, like one that fires only mid-transition, gets through to the render, which then stops at the frame where it fires.
- **Status files.** `render`, `final` and `check` keep `out/<job>.status.json` up to date: `state` (running, done, failed), start and last-update times, progress (chunks done, for render), the last error, and notes such as a skipped gate. Each run ends with a single `DONE <job> <film>` or `FAILED <job> <film>: <reason>` line. A detached run's outcome can be read from the file or the log's last line without a live session. A run killed outright (power loss, `kill -9`) stays at `running`; compare `updated` with the clock.
- **Repeatable frames.** Every page the engine opens (render, stills, check) runs Chrome with `--disable-gpu-rasterization --disable-partial-raster` (`render.RASTER`). With Chrome's defaults, the same moment rendered twice gave different pixels on a page with gradients, filters and grain: on a Mac, 1 of 8 sampled moments differed in 1.82% of its pixels, and a fresh page's first frames differed by 1 to 3 levels. A re-rendered or resumed chunk would then not match the frames around it. With the flags, all 8 moments matched exactly (kit test film, 2026-10-26). `check` renders `--repeat` moments (8, spread through the film; 0 skips) twice, once more in the same page after seeking away and once in a fresh browser, and fails any that differ, with the share of pixels that changed. It costs a few seconds. `MS_GPU_RASTER=1` turns the flags off; check then names the setting next to any difference. Trade-offs: software raster rendered that 39 s gradient-heavy test about 25% slower on the Mac (0.43 s a frame against 0.35 s with one worker); the marker look is untimed. The probe samples 8 moments, so a fault that shows only in some scenes can still get through.
- **Numbers on screen at their word.** `check` finds every amount, count or duration in the narration, using the timed words. That is money, percentages, written numbers, and number words before a noun ("forty porta-potties", "three months"); not "one", years or ordinals. Each needs a matching number visible from 0.3 s before its word to 0.7 s after; the window is 0.7 s, not 0.5, so the counter's and the hero's 0.6 s count-ups can land. A number already on screen 1 s before it is first said fails as early. A segment opts out with `"unshown": true`, a reason, or a list of the values it leaves off. A counter change without an `"at"` word is flagged, since it would change at the segment's start. `--no-numbers` skips the test. Why: the editing study found 6 of 83 spoken amounts on screen within 1 s of their word, and the owner asked "Shouldn't the stats be literally on the screen?" (2026-11-27). Trade-offs: matching is by value within 0.5%, so a rounded figure ("about forty-eight hundred" against $4,860) needs an opt-out or the exact number; a chart that shows all its values before the voice walks through them fails as early unless the segment opts out.
- **One still, one move.** Adjacent panels on the same still (no clip) are one shot: the first panel's camera runs across all of them, so the cut between them is invisible. A camera name on a later panel is ignored, and a camera object starts a new move, which `timeline` warns is a cut back to the same picture. Why: the owner's note on a cut back to the same picture (2026-11-27).
- **T-sheet.** `longform.py tsheet <film> --all` writes the whole film as `review/tsheet.md`; without `--all` it writes the intro only. Spoken numbers are bold in the audio column, numbers on screen are bold in the visual column (including the counter, stat values and callouts), and a spoken number nothing in its segment shows is marked "not on screen".
- **Intro.** Two of the intro rules (SKILL.md, Intros) are checked; the rest are judgement.
  - **Length.** The intro runs 45 s at most (`--intro-max`, 0 skips). The intro is the segments marked `"intro": true`. Without that mark, it is the first chapter when that chapter is called Intro, Cold open, Opening or Hook, or when the film has more than one chapter. A Short is skipped.
  - **Mute test.** The first 5 s (`--mute`) must contain a scene that draws a picture rather than words on a ground. The subject must also be named with the sound off: script.json's `"subject"` (a word or a list, matched whole-word, singular or plural) has to appear on screen, or in the `"shows"` line of a segment in those seconds. `"shows"` says what the still or clip depicts, in plain words ("a row of blue porta-potties at a county fair"). `check` writes `review/mute-0-5s.jpg`, five frames across the first 5 s; hand it to someone who hasn't heard the script.
  - **Trade-off.** The engine can't see what a picture depicts. The mute test trusts `"shows"`, so a wrong `"shows"` line passes, and the contact strip is the real test.
  - **T-sheet.** `longform.py tsheet <film>` writes `review/intro-tsheet.md` (`--all` for the whole film, above): time (once `timeline` has run), segment, audio (the narration) and visual (scene type, still or clip, `shows`, on-screen text). Read it against the promise, the gaps and the levers before boards.
- **Reading time.** `check` lists every piece of on-screen text in `review/text-sheet.tsv` with when it is fully shown and how long it holds, and fails a block held under max(2 s, 1 s + words / 3), or under 3 s for a segment marked `"payoff": true` (its reveal is the point of the scene) or text still up when a chapter cuts. Lengthen the segment (`extra`), land the reveal on an earlier word, or cut words. Text under 3:1 against the pixels behind it is flagged, with how long it was readable (critic.md, text contrast). A moment when the block is mid-fade (drawn below 0.95 opacity, its own and every parent's) is left out, so an exit fade isn't flagged as low contrast; text that is never drawn fully opaque is measured at its most opaque moment. The palette is linted too, by `timeline` (a WARN line) and by `check` (a flag): ACCENT, INK or GREY under the contrast floor (3:1) against BG is named with the nearest colour of the same hue that reaches 3.5:1. A light ground must set GREY too: the house grey is made for a dark ground and is 2.1:1 on a warm paper. Why: stat numbers in a pale gold on warm paper measured 2.0:1 and only the frame sampler found it (2026-11-27). See critic.md, text hold.
- **Pacing.** `check` flags dead air (the voice pausing over 1.5 s with 3 words or fewer on screen and nothing moving), a pose keyed twice in a row, a figure that only idles while the voice speaks over 3 s, and a beat with nothing new moving or entering while the voice speaks over 5 s. A segment meant to stay on one beat takes `"steady": true` (a kit element, `data-hold`). These are flags for judgement. See critic.md, dead air, pose variety and beat length.
- **Review before render.** `render` on a 9:16 film stops until a `stills` contact sheet newer than `timeline.js` exists in `review/`. Look at it before rendering.

Trade-offs: each Short needs a layout and review pass instead of a free crop. A Short has to be a contiguous run of segments, because its sound is one cut from the long-form mix. A panel still is enlarged to 1920 tall, so a 9:16 panel is softer than the same panel in 16:9; draw panels meant for Shorts at 2K. The safe-area numbers are from YouTube's layout as of 2026-10 and may change.

## Music by mood

`music` measures every library track once (loudness, onset density, pulse strength, brightness, major/minor lean; stored in `catalog.json` as `mood`) and ranks tracks against each chapter's mood: `tension`, `mystery`, `serious`, `calm`, `warm`, `drive`, `uplift` (targets in `audio.MOODS`). Consecutive chapters with one mood share a track; a mood change brings a different track on a 3 s crossfade at the chapter line; the last track is back-timed so its own ending lands on the film's end. A run whose best fit is under 0.6 prints NO GOOD FIT with search words for the library source: fetch better tracks rather than shipping a club track under a tense chapter. Edit `track` in `out/music_plan.json` to override. Music sits at -25.5 dB under the voice (the channel owner found -24 a little loud) and ducks further while it speaks. Never put music with vocals under narration.

## UI sounds

`mix` adds one sound per on-screen event, read from the timeline: a buzz when a phone lights (`glow`), a ping when a message lands (`notify`, an `sms` beat or scene; 0.12 s after the buzz when both happen), a paper tick when a board card pins, a thud when a stamp hits. They sit at -12 dB (`--sfx-db`) under the voice. Same-kind sounds closer than the gaps in `SFX_GAP` are dropped so a burst of cards doesn't rattle. Each kind uses recorded library clips that carry `"role": "buzz" | "ping" | "pin" | "thud"` in `catalog.json` (cycled, level varied ±15%, transient on the cue). A film sets its own default per kind in script.json: `"ui_sounds": {"ping": false, "buzz": {"gain": 0.2, "lowpass": 2500}}` turns a kind off, or scales it (amplitude) and low-passes it (Hz, 4th-order Butterworth); the toll-text owner wanted no bell and a buzz that is felt more than heard. A kind with no recording stays silent and `mix` says so; `--sfx-drawn` fills it with a sound drawn in code, as a placeholder only: the channel owner liked that the sounds landed on their events in the toll-text film but found the drawn sounds themselves amateur. Sounds must be professional recordings or generations that are free for commercial use (sound.md).

## Review dashboard: takes, picks and section locks

The studio preview (`python3 scripts/studio.py serve <film>`) is the owner's review dashboard for a long-form film. Send him its link with every finished batch, so his picks and his notes come back in one pass.

- **Only checked takes reach him** (picture-truth.md rule 16.4). A take made after 2026-11-27 shows in the takes view only once it has passed the full-size check: `studio.py gate <film> <take path> --question "<the note's own question>" --pass` (or `--fail --why "..."`). `--ruler x,ground_y,ref_y,ref_ft` first draws a foot ruler on a full-size copy, and `studio.py gate <film> --list` lists the takes still waiting. A shot with takes held back says "N takes waiting for the full-size check", and he can choose "also unchecked" to see them dimmed. A take redrawn under the same name drops back to unchecked.

- **Takes.** A panel still or clip named `<shot>_s<seed>.<ext>` with siblings `<shot>_s<other seed>.<ext>` is a take group. The header's **takes** button (key T) lays the takes side by side; he marks one with "use this". The pick goes to `<film>/picks.json` as `{"<shot>": <seed>}` (`"<shot>:clip"` when the shot has both still and clip takes). `longform.py timeline` swaps each picked take into the film; `check` stops when picks.json is newer than timeline.js and lists the groups still waiting.
- **Agents never pick among takes and never lock a section.** Both are his clicks only (his rule, 2026-11-26). Make the takes, then send the link.
- **Sections.** Each chapter's band on the timeline is coloured by state, first match wins: locked (green), not started (grey), takes to pick (amber), open notes (orange), in progress (blue), ready to lock (teal). The Section panel shows why and holds the lock button. A lock is stored in `<film>/review/state.json` with a stamp of the chapter's segments and picks; if either changes afterwards the band gets a stripe, `check` reports it, and the section needs his re-lock.
- **Notes** work as before: drag a circle on the frame, pin the note at the playhead.
- **Finding takes.** The takes view filters by type (stills or clips), by the panel's shot (EST, WIDE, CU and so on, when the script gives one) and by state (waiting for a pick, picked, with open comments, with new takes). It sorts by last updated (the newest take file or comment, the default) or by film order. A take whose file is newer than his last visit carries a "new" badge, and the header button counts them; closing the view or "mark all seen" clears them. Filters, sort and the seen time are kept in that browser. Clip takes show their first frame as a poster (`/api/poster`, cached in review/posters/), and every take URL carries its file's mtime, so a take redrawn under the same name never shows the old picture.
- **Picking a comment up.** When you start on a take comment, run `studio.py notes <film> --ack N --reply "redrawing as seed 5"` straight away. The note stays open (it still counts against its section), and its card shows "seen · redrawing as seed 5", so he can see it was picked up. Resolve it with `--resolve N --reply "..."` once the new take is in. A comment with no sign of pickup read to him as ignored (2026-11-26).
- **Notes on a take.** Each take card in the takes view has its own comment box. That note is pinned to the take (`take: {key, seed, n, label}` in notes.json, its still is the take's own file, its time the shot's start), shows in the notes list as "m0c · take 2", and `studio.py notes <film>` prints it as "on m0c · take 2 (seed 2, <path>)" with the file to open. Read every take note before redrawing or picking up that shot; a note on a take he didn't pick may still say what the redraw must keep or fix.
- **On his phone:** `studio.py serve <film> --lan` binds every interface on the Mac and prints two links: `http://<LocalHostName>.local:<port>/?k=<key>` (the Bonjour name from `scutil --get LocalHostName`, not the computer name) and the Wi-Fi IP as a fallback for when the name doesn't open. When the Mac is on Tailscale (`tailscale ip -4` answers), the Tailscale link is printed first: it works on a Wi-Fi that isolates clients (the owner's building does) and from anywhere, as long as the phone is on the same tailnet, and its traffic is encrypted, unlike the plain-HTTP Wi-Fi links. The phone needs the Tailscale app signed in to the same account. Trade-off: anyone on the same wifi who has that link can pick, lock and write notes. The key (random, kept in `review/.lan-key`, sent as a cookie after the first visit) is the only guard, the traffic is plain HTTP so it is readable on that network, and it only works while phone and Mac share the network. Without `--lan` the server listens on localhost only.

## Sharing a draft

`share` turns `out/<film>.mp4` into `out/share/index.html` (poster, chapter buttons, the script's palette accent) plus `out/share/v/`. Publish the page with the Artifact tool and every file in `v/` under its own path; the result is private until the owner shares it from the page's Share menu, and a viewer just presses play. Why it is built this way: the artifact host serves neither `.m3u8` nor `.ts`, caps each file at 15 MB and each publish at 64 MB, and loads scripts only from approved CDNs, so the cut goes out as fMP4 HLS segments named `.mp4` with the playlist renamed `index.txt`, played through hls.js from cdnjs. Segment length comes from the cut's average bitrate: about 10 MB of video at that rate, between 4 and 60 s, which leaves room for busier stretches and for each cut landing on the next keyframe. If a segment still comes out over 14 MB, `share` halves the length and splits again, up to four times. `--segment N` sets the starting length. A 10-minute 1080p cut is about 51 MB. Trade-off: a busy cut makes more, smaller files, so a long one can need more than one publish (it prints when it is over 60 MB). It needs Media Source Extensions: desktop browsers and iOS 17.1 or later; older iPhones get an error line. Optional `"share": {"headline", "lede", "eyebrow", "foot", "poster_at"}` in script.json sets the page text. The container's Playwright Chromium has no H.264 decoder, so a local test can only confirm that the page loads the playlist and segments, not that it plays; have the owner open it once before it goes to anyone.

## Lessons from the toll-text build

Each of these came from the channel owner watching a cut; the fix lives in the engine, not in one film.

- **Captions must follow the voice, not the page.** Long caption pages let the eye run ahead and word timing drifted up to 0.9 s after pauses. Fixed by short pages (about 4 words), word edges snapped to measured silence, and trying both readings of a year. Check any new voice on its first film: median word-start error should stay near 0.
- **Music under narration is quieter than it sounds while building.** -24 dB was too loud on a phone speaker; -25.5 dB is the default, with every track level-matched first so a quiet track doesn't jump at a chapter change.
- **Sound placement works; the sounds must be professional.** One sound per visible event, never per animation, from recorded or generated clips that are free for commercial use. Drawn-in-code sounds read as amateur.
- **A mood measure can't tell relative keys apart.** Composed minor beds read as major; trust chapter intent and override `out/music_plan.json` by hand.
- **Music sources may be blocked from the build machine.** Check the library can be reached before planning a film around new tracks (sound.md lists the routes).
- **The owner reviews on a link, not a file.** Run `share` with every draft so review doesn't wait on a download or a YouTube upload.

## Traps hit building this

- Two camera keys at the same time divide by zero and the camera jumps a whole frame. Build keys with `camKeys()`, which keeps them 0.8 s apart. It also caps camera speed: no glide averages more than 1300 screen px/s (`CAM_VMAX`; the eased peak is three times that), so a long move glides for up to 2.2 s (`CAM_GMAX`) instead of 0.9 s, and lands late when the gap since the last key is too short for it. Values are screen pixels, so a kit calling it at zoom z passes `vmax / z`. The Trapdoor kit's 2,400 px whip in 0.9 s smeared (2026-10-24). The engine's own scenes lean less than 800 px per key, so only kit cameras reach the cap.
- An opaque scene that doesn't paint every pixel shows the previous scene through its gaps until that one leaves, which reads as a pop. Opaque scenes get a full-frame backdrop automatically; keep it if you write a new one.
- Fixed labels on a scene with a moving camera drift off frame: put them in `s.ov`, the per-segment overlay that the camera doesn't move.
- **Text shimmered under the camera.** A slow zoom (the 1.0 to 1.03 drift every scene had, and the stage, board and screenshot pushes) re-rasterises every glyph each frame, so line gaps jittered by up to 2 px. The owner saw it as bubble text "blinking" on its line spacing; a screenshot can't show it. Fixed by dropping zoom from every scene except the drama `panel` (whose text sits in `s.ov`), landing unscaled pans on whole pixels, and letting springs (`K.step`) settle to exactly 1. Camera leans toward the newest card, speaker or mark remain, as whole-pixel pans. Check text stability by measuring line gaps across consecutive frames, not by eye on stills.
- `verify` treats a wipe crossing a bright figure, or a stamp, as a pop. `final` passes those windows as allowed; anything outside them is a real glitch.
