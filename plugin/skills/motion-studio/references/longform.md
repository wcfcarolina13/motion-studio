# Long-form narrated films

For 5–20 minute 16:9 explainers (YouTube). The narration is the clock: each segment of the script gets one voice clip, the clip's length sets the segment's length, and anything in a scene can be timed to a spoken word. Run everything with `L=${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/longform.py`.

## Pipeline

| Step | Command | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| scaffold | `python3 $L init <film>` | film.html (engine), kit.js, figure.js, closeup.js, folders |
| voice | `python3 $L lines <film>` → one mp3 per segment id in `narration/` (any TTS or a human read). It prints what the voice should say, with the `say` map applied, and lists likely misreads on stderr as `SAY:` lines; fix them before voicing. After voicing, `python3 $L voiced <film>` records each clip's voice, pack, speed, words and hash; `timeline` stops on a clip that is unrecorded, changed since, voiced for other words, or voiced with another voice, pack or speed | `narration/s01.mp3` …, `narration/voice.json` |
| word times | `python3 $L align <film>` | `narration/words.json` (offline forced alignment) |
| timeline | `python3 $L timeline <film>` | `timeline.js`, `out/narration.wav`, `out/chapters.txt`, `out/captions.vtt` (`captions --lang es --text es.json` adds `out/captions.es.vtt`) |
| look | `python3 $L stills <film> --every 2.5` | stills + contact sheet in `review/` |
| music | `python3 $L music <film>` then `python3 $L mix <film> --plan` then `python3 $L credits <film>` | `out/music_plan.json`, `out/mix.wav`, with UI sounds from the timeline (below; `--no-sfx` leaves them out) and the library's whoosh at chapter starts (`--no-whoosh`); `out/credits.txt` for the description. `mix --plan` stops when the plan no longer matches the timeline's chapters or end (run `music` again), and when the bed goes quiet (30 dB under its usual level) more than 3 s before the film ends; the last track runs under the tail and end screen |
| check | `python3 $L check <film>` | `review/check.txt`, `review/text-sheet.tsv`, `review/check-gate.json` (see `critic.md`); render needs a passing page gate on this version of the film. Run it again after `render` for the dead-air, cue and flash tests |
| render | `python3 $L render <film> --workers 4` | `out/silent.mp4` |
| ship | `python3 $L final <film>` | `out/<film>.mp4`, verify report |
| upload | `python3 ${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/youtube.py upload … --privacy private` | private video; `captions` adds the VTT; going public and the 48-hour review: `publish.md` |
| share a draft | `python3 $L share <film>` | `out/share/`: a page that streams the cut, to publish as a private artifact (below) |
| Short | `python3 $L short <film> <short> --from s05 --to s09`, then lay it out, `timeline`, `stills`, `render`, `final` in the Short's folder | a 9:16 film that reuses the long-form voice and sound (below) |

`align` needs `pip install pocketsphinx` (its English model ships in the wheel, no download). Without it, words are spread across each clip by spoken weight between measured pauses, good to about 0.15 s. Numbers, money and years are expanded the way a narrator says them ("$4.12" → four dollars twelve cents) before aligning; words the dictionary doesn't know take the gap between their neighbours. A year is tried both as "twenty twenty-five" and "two thousand twenty-five" (voices differ), keeping the reading whose word starts land on the measured pauses, and word edges are then snapped onto measured silence (the aligner otherwise hands leading silence to the first word).

## script.json

```json
{"title": "…", "moods": {"Intro": "mystery", "The trick": "tension"}, "captions": false, "type_scale": 1,
 "segments": [
  {"id": "s01", "chapter": "Intro", "type": "stage", "source": "…", "narration": "You've probably had this text…",
   "data": {"set": "room", "cast": [{"id": "you", "x": 0.36, "skin": 2, "hair": "short", "shirt": "#e8772e"}],
            "beats": [{"who": "you", "at": "text", "pose": "phone", "glow": true, "notify": "TOLL NOTICE: …"},
                      {"who": "you", "at": "late fee", "pose": "read", "emote": "!"}]}}]}
```

**Text size.** Reading text in every scene type is at least `READ` (56 px wide, 40 px in a Short), so it holds up on a phone, and `check` fails anything smaller. `"type_scale": 1.2` scales that floor and the list size for one film. The list carries the advice, so its items are 66 px. A text message is drawn at a phone's proportions, so once it lands the camera pushes in (and holds still) until the message reads at the floor. A stage notification sits beside the phone hand at head height, or the nearest clear spot (lower, higher, the other side, overhead) that covers no one else's body, bubble or the kicker. Trade-offs: bigger text means fewer words per line, so long list items and board cards wrap onto more lines, and board cards are larger (440 px wide); a board with many cards needs more room between them. A board's stamp sits under its card's words, or over the card's top edge when the board ends first, and `check` fails any text that something is painted over (critic.md, overlap).

**One voice per film.** `"voice"` in script.json (default: Kokoro `am_michael`, model pack v1.0, speed 1.0, the owner's pick on 2026-10-23) applies to every clip; a drama sets `"voices": {"<speaker>": {...}}`. A human read is `{"engine": "human"}`. Voice each clip with exactly those settings, then run `voiced` (`voiced <film> s05 --speed 1.17` records an exception honestly, and `timeline` then names it). `check` also flags any clip spoken more than 15% faster or slower than the film's median pace (spoken letters per second, from the word times), which catches a speed slip even when nothing was recorded; a 1.17 re-voice showed as +37% on the test film. It also flags a passage: three or more clips in a row that are each at least 5% off the median in the same direction (toll-text's opening ran 6 to 11% fast across its first scenes and sounded rushed, under the single-clip limit, 2026-10-24). Re-voice those clips or loosen the read. A Short copies its clips' records. Trade-offs: the record trusts whoever runs `voiced` about the settings (only the words and the file are checked), so the pace check is the backstop; it cannot tell two voices of the same pace apart, and a film voiced before this needs one `voiced` run before `timeline` will go.

**What the voice says.** The narration is written for the screen and the captions. When the voice must be given something else, `"say"` maps a written phrase to its spoken form, film-wide at the top of script.json and per segment (`"say": {"John Does": "John Doughs", "ezpay-toll.example": "ezpay toll dot example"}`). `lines` hands the TTS the spoken form; captions and word cues keep the written words, and `align` times the spoken words back onto them (word for word when the counts match, else all on the phrase's first word). `lines` and `check` lint the spoken text for: known misreads (`say.KNOWN`; Kokoro reads the surname plural "Does" as the verb, toll-text 2026-10-23), web addresses, phone-style numbers, long digit strings without commas, letter-digit codes, and times or dates in figures. Amounts, short numbers, years and ordinals pass. Add a `KNOWN` entry whenever a listen-through catches a new misread. Misreads with a fix that holds in every sentence go in `say.DEFAULT_SAY` instead, which every film gets under its own map (a film entry for the same phrase wins): it splits "twenty-eight" through "ninety-eight" into two words, because Kokoro runs the hyphenated form together (toll-text, 2026-10-24). A film voiced before a default was added stops at `timeline` with "voiced for other words" on the clips it changes; re-voice those clips. Words spelt one way and said two ways are `say.HOMOGRAPHS`, in two tiers.
- **Block tier.** These have both readings in the same slot in narration, so the voice's part-of-speech guess is a coin toss: live, lives, read, lead(s), wind(s), tear(s), wound, bow(s), row(s), bass. `lines` and `voiced` refuse a line containing one until it is rewritten or pinned. To pin a reading, give the phrase misaki's inline phonemes in the say map, for example `"say": {"barely live": "barely [live](/lˈIv/)"}`. In misaki's alphabet, A is eɪ, I is aɪ, O is oʊ, W is aʊ and Y is ɔɪ. Captions keep the plain word, and alignment strips the markup. The owner's rule is to avoid "live" in any form (2026-10-24), so prefer the rewrite.
- **Warn tier.** These are noun/verb stress pairs and similar words, where the part of speech usually settles the reading: close, record(s), present, object, minute, use, content, invalid, desert, refuse, produce. `lines` lists them as LISTEN and `check` reports them for the listen-through without failing.
- **Not listed.** "Living" has one pronunciation.

`--allow-homographs` hands a line over anyway, for one that a listen-through has confirmed reads right. Trade-offs: refusing a common word like "use" would stop most films, which is why it is only a warning. A pinned word is read exactly as written, so a wrong phoneme string is read wrong every time, and pinning only works through a voice runner that parses misaki's markup. Trade-off: the lint is pattern-based and never hears the audio, so it misses misreads it has no rule for, and a name like "Mary Does" in a title-case line can be flagged when it is the verb.

**Word cues.** Any object inside `data` with `"at": "<word or phrase>"` gets an absolute time `t` when `timeline` runs (`"nth": 2` for a repeat, `"offset": -0.1` to lead). Things that must be read land 0.05 s before their word. `timeline` prints a PROBLEM line for any cue word that isn't in the narration. List items, flow nodes, hub spokes, compare items and statement lines accept `{"text": …, "at": …}` in place of a plain string; `stat` and `sms` take `"at"` on their data to start the count or the message on a word, and `stat` takes `"notes": [{"text", "at"}]`, follow-up lines under the label, so a long stat scene doesn't sit still after the count.

## Scene types

Text-led (use sparingly; `check` flags a film that is more than 40% these): `title`, `chapter`, `statement`, `quote`, `list`.
Diagrams: `compare`, `stat`, `bars`, `flow`, `hub`, `sms` (a phone with a message).

- **`compare`**: two columns, `left` and `right`, each `{title, icon?, items}`. Both headings (and icons: `phone`, `bank`, `card`, `person`, `money`, `web`, `doc`, `x`, `check`, in a ring above the heading) are up in the first second, and items land row by row, left then right, across the first 70% of the segment (or on their `at` words), so both sides stay on screen while the viewer compares. Long items wrap; a row is as tall as its taller side. `accent: "left"` moves the highlight off the right column. `strike: true` crosses out each left item as its right item lands, for "instead of" pairs; it is off by default because striking the real side of a real-versus-fake pair reads as wrong (it was always on before 2026-10-23). A side's `color` (`"red"`, `"green"` or a hex) tints its ring, icon and heading, and `fill: true` makes the ring a solid disc with the icon in white. Red and green come from the palette's `RED` and `GREEN` when it has them, otherwise #e0483e and #3fae5a, both of which read on dark and light grounds. For a do/don't pair the owner wants a bold green tick and a red cross (2026-10-23): `{"icon": "check", "color": "green", "fill": true}` and `{"icon": "x", "color": "red", "fill": true}`. Trade-off: red and green alone fail for red-green colour-blind viewers, so the tick and cross shapes carry the meaning and the colour only reinforces it. In a Short the columns stack.
Evidence: `capture`, a captured screenshot of a real page or message (below, under real things on screen). `data.real` names the capture. `frame` is `browser` (the default, with the page's address in the bar), `phone` or `none`. `scroll: [from, to]` moves down a tall capture over the segment (0 to 1 of what doesn't fit). `marks: [{box: [x, y, w, h], at, label}]` draws accent boxes on the image (0 to 1 of the image) on their words, and the camera leans toward the newest one. The footer shows the segment's `source`, or else the capture's `credit`, or else the site's domain.
People and evidence (their own set; they wipe on and off left to right instead of cutting). The wipe's edge is laid out on the frame, not in the scene, so a scene whose camera has panned or zoomed still fills the frame to its edges; the stage set's wall and floor run half a frame past each side, more than the camera's widest lean (a figure at x 0.12 used to leave a 55 px unpainted strip at the left edge, Longtok 2026-10-25):

- **`stage`**: rigged figures on a set (`room`, `street`, `desk`, `blank`). Cast entries take figure options (below) plus `x` (0–1), `h` (px, default 540), `pose`, `enter: "left"|"right"` (walks in). Beats: `pose`, `face`, `emote` (`? ! $ … x`), `say` / `think` (bubble; `""` clears it with a 0.3 s fade), `notify` (a message card beside the phone; `from` changes its "MESSAGES · now" header, e.g. for a browser or an app), `sms` (the text message itself coming off the phone: `sender`, default "+1 (555) 010-0199", and `senderNote`, default "Text Message", above the body in a grey bubble, laid out like the `sms` scene; it is placed like `notify` and keeps clear of a notification already up), `glow` (the phone lights and buzzes), `move` (walk to a new x). The camera leans toward whoever acted last, panning by fractions of a pixel (`g.__cam.frac`; on whole pixels a slow ease stepped the set every other frame). Nothing on a stage changes in a single frame, because final's pop check fails that. Pose changes anticipate when the film's `anticipate` is on: a lean back before a big pose change, an overshoot that settles, and an optional `hold` (frames) on a pose beat (the `realise` pose holds 2). `init` writes `engine.json` with `"anticipate": true`, so films made from 2026-10-25 on get it. A film with no `engine.json` (any film made before) keeps the old moves, so its re-renders don't change, and `"anticipate"` in script.json overrides either way. A Short copies its film's `engine.json`. Bubbles and cards hang from the plain move, so their text moves no more than before. Trade-off: the lean back starts 0.12 s before the word, so a pose beat's first motion now comes slightly early. A bubble that replaces another waits while the old one fades for 0.22 s, so it lands 0.22 s after its cue. A change of facing, whether from a `face` beat or from setting off on or finishing a walk, turns over 0.2 s, and the bubbles slide across with it. A notification or `sms` is placed clear of every bubble that will be up while it shows, including ones still to come, so it never jumps when one appears or goes. A card keeps off every head (weight 4) and leans on its holder's torso less readily than on the holder's legs. Words under a card are lost, so the kicker counts as heavily as a bubble (weight 3), and on a Short no card rises above the safe area's top. A card tries its usual wrap, a narrower one (up to 6 lines, for a spot beside a figure near the frame edge) and a wider one (for a spot overhead), and takes a reflow only when it covers clearly less; a reflow never drops words. When a scene has no clear spot, for example a long message in an upright two-shot, check fails it as "card over <id>'s head" or "card over <id>'s body" (a quarter of anyone's torso). Shorten the message, move the cast, or use the `sms` scene instead (Longtok found a card covering its holder from the head down, then one over its holder's torso and legs, 2026-10-24). Trade-offs: a card may now change shape between scenes, and keeping cards clear of the kicker and the Short's top edge leaves fewer spots, so a few more scenes fail check. These come from the Trapdoor kit's stage_smooth (2026-10-24). `kicker` is a fixed label top left. `light: "left"|"right"` lights the set from that side: a warm pool on that side, a falloff to dark on the far side (over the figures too, under bubbles and cards), and a soft shadow on the floor under every prop and figure, cast away from the light. `foreground: true` puts dark, out-of-focus shapes on the frame's bottom corners, in front of the action and below knee height (an armchair and a tall plant in a room, a chair back and a desk lamp at a desk, a railing and a bollard on the street, a low ledge on blank). Every piece stops at about knee height, and each corner's pieces slide outward, off the frame if they must, to stay clear of every figure's box widened by 12% each side, wherever that figure stands or walks to in the scene. They also keep clear of the scene's source line (bottom left on a wide film) wherever the camera goes in the scene, plus a pad that takes in a soft piece's blur: a corner piece slides outward, usually off the frame, and the blank set's ledge stops above the line. `check` fails any text drawn straight onto a foreground piece ("on the stage foreground"), because the piece's edges run through the words; posts and furniture through the source line made it hard to read on a phone (Longtok, 2026-10-25). Trade-off: a scene with a source loses its left foreground piece on a wide film. They sit on the corners where the camera rests on average and slide 35% further than the set when it pans, so the scene reads as having depth. Both are off unless set, so older films render the same. `look: "flat"` draws both for a flat, outlined art style such as a marker-on-paper kit: each shadow is a hard-edged shape in one darker tone of the floor (`shadow: "#hex"` sets it, for a kit that paints its own floor) at 25% opacity, offset away from the light by 6% of the object's height, with no blur and no light pool or falloff; the foreground pieces are solid shapes in that tone with an outline in `ink: "#hex"` (default the palette's `INK`), unblurred, running off the frame's bottom edge (Longtok, 2026-10-25). The film's vignette is a grade, not part of the stage: a flat film sets `grade.vignette` to 0. Trade-offs: the far side of a lit set is darker, so a figure there reads a little dimmer. On the engine's own dark sets a flat shadow barely shows, because a darker tone of a near-black floor is nearly the same colour; the flat look is for light floors. The foreground covers the feet of a figure standing at the frame's edge. The lamp drawn in a room stands on the right whatever `light` says. The shapes are fixed per set and not yet configurable. Each set is dressed so the place reads at a glance: `room` has a window with a moon, a framed picture, a sofa with a cushion, a lamp and a plant; `desk` has a monitor on a stand, keyboard, mug, papers, a drawer unit and a plant; `street` has buildings with lit windows and a street lamp. `screen: {url, title?, lines?}` puts a browser page on the desk's monitor. The monitor is drawn at a monitor's size beside a person, so its text is set dressing and too small to read; say what matters in a `notify` or bubble too (notifications keep clear of the screen). A kit that draws its own layer into a stage scene (in the segment's group `g` or in `s.ov`) marks what notifications must avoid with a `data-clear` attribute on any element, its value naming it for check (`data-clear="hand mech"`). Each marked element is measured every frame, moving or not, so placement steers around it and check's overlap test fails text that lands on it. This replaces patching the stage builder (the Trapdoor kit's `window.STAGE_CLEAR`, 2026-10-24). The owner could not tell what the old blank trapezoid monitor was (2026-10-23).
- **Stage theme** (`"theme"` in script.json): every colour the `stage` and `sms` scenes draw with, so a kit restyles them without patching the template. Groups, each merged over the house defaults (a film without `theme` renders exactly as before): `set` (`wall`, `wallStreet`, `floor`, `skirting`, `wood`, `dark`, `line`, `pot`, `leaf`, `leafLight`, `glow`, `lampShade`, `building`, `buildingAlt`, `windowLit`, `windowDark`, `post`, `glass`, `windowFrame`, `moon`, `picture`, `pictureFrame`, `pictureArt`, `sofa`, `cushion`, `drawer`, `bezel`, `screen`, `sheen`, `page`, `pageBar`, `pageDot`, `pageUrl`, `pageInk`, `pageLine`, `keyboard`, `mug`, `paper`, `paperAlt`), `light` (`falloff`, `warm`), `shadow` (`soft` and `softOpacity` for the soft look; `tone` and `opacity` for the flat look; `blend`), `fore` (`soft`; `fill`, `ink`, `outline` px for the flat look, where 0 draws no outline), `bubble` (`fill`, `text`) and `card` (`phone`, `stroke`, `screen`, `box`, `bubble`, `reply`, `replyText`, `text`, `sub`) for the phone cards. Any value is an SVG paint, so a gradient the kit defines (`"floor": "url(#kit-floor)"`) works. The flat shadow tone defaults to the floor darkened by half, or black when the floor is not a plain hex colour. `"blend": "multiply"` darkens whatever lies under a shadow by the same proportion, so one tone reads right across a gradient floor. A scene's own `shadow` and `ink` still win over the theme. A misspelt group or key stops the page with "theme: unknown ...", rather than quietly keeping the house colour. Trade-off: a theme that lightens the card's `box` must also set its `text`, which stays the palette's `INK` otherwise (the overlap check does not measure contrast).
- **Stage hooks** (for kits): define `window.STAGE_HOOKS` in a script that loads before the template's own script. `backdrop(kind, group, {gy, W, H, data, theme})` is called once per stage scene with the backdrop group, which holds the default wall (or the street's sky colour) under every prop. Draw over it, or call `group.replaceChildren()` first to replace it (a sky gradient, a skyline, wallpaper). `set(kind, g, set, data)` is called once the set is drawn, before the cast. `set.lights` lists the set's light sources, each `{kind, x, y, w, h?, top}` in the scene's own units, which pan with the camera. `kind` is `lamp` (the room's lamp), `streetLamp`, `window` (the room's window) or `monitor` (the desk's screen). `x`, `y` is the point to hang a light cone or glow from: the underside of a lamp's shade or the street lamp's head, or the middle of a window or screen. Draw into `g` there and it sits behind the cast. The same object is on the scene group as `g.__set` for a kit that works after the build. Neither hook exists by default, so a film without them renders as before.
- **`board`**: an evidence board. `cards` (`id, x, y, title, text, icon: phone|bank|card|person|money|web|doc, kind: "note"`) pin up on their words; `strings` (`from, to`) draw red string; `stamp` (`on, text`) slams onto a card. The camera drifts toward the newest card.
- **`crowd`**: rows of seated people on phones; the camera starts on one person and pulls back to the room (`from`/`to` are 0–1 of the segment), then `label` and an optional `count` (`value, prefix, suffix, label, at`).

- **`panel`**: one composed illustration per beat, for drama (webtoon / motion comic): establishing shots, close-ups, inserts, two-shots and flashbacks drawn as full frames, never one cutout over one background (YouTube's "image slideshow" pattern). `data.image` is the still (16:9, at least the frame size; larger leaves room to move). The camera crops and scales it, so the frame stays covered. `shot` (`EST` `WIDE` `MED` `CU` `ECU` `INS` `OTS` `TWO`) picks the default move: EST pulls back, WIDE and OTS pan toward `focus`, MED/CU/ECU/INS push in toward `focus` ([x, y], 0–1 of the frame, default [0.5, 0.45]). `camera` overrides it with `push` `pull` `pan-left` `pan-right` `tilt-up` `tilt-down` `hold`, or `{from: {x, y, z}, to: {x, y, z}}`. `flashback: true` grades sepia with a heavy vignette. `caption_card` (`"Three days earlier"` or `{text, at, hold}`) is a boxed caption top left. `freeze` (`true` or `{at}`) stops the camera and drains the colour (a cliffhanger freeze). `shake: {at, amp}` is a decaying jolt on an impact. Panels cut by default; `enter`/`exit: "fade"` fades through black. Several beats may reuse one image with a different `shot`, `focus` or `camera`. Write the generator's `prompt` and `char_in_frame` on the panel's first beat; `longform.py panels <film>` lists each unique image once with its prompt, characters and whether it exists (`out/panels.json`, for the image runner). Panels come from the image model at 1344x768. `longform.py upscale <film>` runs Real-ESRGAN (`realesrgan-ncnn-vulkan`, BSD-3, anime model) at 4x on each one, then Lanczos down to cover 2304x1296 and centre-crops, writing to `panels-hd/` at the same relative path. 2304x1296 is the frame times the deepest camera push (ECU, 1.2), so a push-in still shows one image pixel per screen pixel. `timeline` uses the upscaled copy when one is newer than its source, and lists any panel under 1920 wide that has none. On the K-factory test (2026-10-17) this took 7.5 s a panel, against 1,541 s for a native 1920x1088 render, and gave sharper lines. A drama segment also takes `speaker` (whose voice reads the line; `lines --json` lists them for TTS), `mono: true` (inner monologue: italic in the caption track, and warm italic if captions are burned in) and `sfx` (foley by kind on a cue word: `["paper slap", {"kind": "low hit", "at": "envelope"}]`; a kind with no recorded library clip stays silent). `bed` (`"rain glass"` or a list) loops an ambience clip under the whole segment, about 10 dB under the hits; back-to-back segments with the same bed share one continuous run, which fades in and out over 0.8 s at its edges. Kind names are one role however they are spelled: "paper slap", "Paper_Slap" and "paper-slap" all match the library role `paper-slap`. Hits are cut to the time their kind may ring (`SFX_MAX`: a gavel 3 s, a slap 0.8 s, unknown kinds 1.5 s); beds are never cut.

**Panel quality** (from K-factory's anatomy research, 2026-10-17). Small step-distilled image models break hands, limbs and busy scenes. The best licence-safe vision judge agreed with people only about 60% of the time, and mflux 0.23 has no masked inpainting for klein or Qwen-Edit. So the engine lints prompts before generation, a person marks faults on a sheet after it, and each fault is repaired by redrawing a crop.
- `longform.py lint <film>` (or a JSON list of panel records with `prompt`, `shot`, `refs`, `plate`) warns on: more than 2 reference images (`data.refs` plus `data.plate`); more than 80 words of scene description (sentences like "Image 1 is the identity reference for …", a last sentence shared by at least half the prompts, and any sentence matching `--boiler REGEX` don't count); more than 2 characters in frame; more than 2 visible hands; contact between two people's hands or arms; "while", "then", "as he" or "idle" (maybe more than one action per character); a plate on a CU, ECU or INS shot; and negative phrasing, which klein ignores because it has no negative prompt ("no text" is left alone). Hand and action counts are heuristics: hands hidden in a pocket, a lap, behind the back or out of frame are not counted, and repeated mentions of one person's hand count once, but a pronoun and a name for the same person count twice. `panels` prints how many prompts have warnings.
- `longform.py review <film> [--checks checks.json]` writes `out/review/index.html`, with every panel numbered in film order. Open it from the film folder. Click a panel to mark it, drag on it to box a region, and type what is wrong in its note. The page keeps the marks in that browser, downloads them as `marks.json`, and prints a `repair` command for each box, with the note as its prompt. `--checks` is any checker's output, `{image: {"verdict": "FAIL", "reason", "boxes": [[x, y, w, h] in 0-1]}}`, drawn as red outlines. A checker only orders the queue; the person decides.
- `longform.py repair <film> --beat b07 --box x,y,w,h --prompt "Redraw the right hand in image 1: thumb on top, four fingers wrapped around the glass, five fingers; keep the cuff, lineart and colours unchanged"`. The box is in panel pixels, or 0-1 as the sheet gives it. The command crops a square 2.25 times the box's longer side (at least 640 px), moved to stay inside the panel, and redraws it at its own size (a crop over 1024 is scaled down to 1024). A resize there and back halves the fine detail: a Lanczos round trip alone measured 0.48 of the panel's detail, which is most of the softness K-factory saw in klein repairs. `--gen-size` forces a size, and a crop enlarged that way goes through Real-ESRGAN first when it is installed (`$REALESRGAN_BIN`, `"realesrgan"` in `~/.motion-studio.json`, or PATH). The default model is Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 at 6-bit (Apache-2.0), because klein barely changes a large box, at about 3 to 6 minutes a seed. `--model klein` (klein 4B, Apache-2.0, about 45 to 75 s a seed) suits a small colour or line fix. `--ref` adds a second image, such as the sleeve and hand cropped from the character sheet. One run makes three seeds by default (`--seeds`).
- The paste covers the box plus `--margin` (32 px), so a redraw that spills a little past the box is kept. It blends over `--feather` (16 px) inside that edge, rising from 0 at the edge with a smoothstep, so nothing outside the paste changes and the box itself is fully replaced. Sides on the panel border get no blend. Before pasting, the redraw's colours are matched to the original on a 20 px band around the paste (per-channel gain and offset, fitted twice so new content in the band does not skew it, gain held to 0.8-1.25); `--no-color-match` turns it off. Each seed is checked and the checks are logged in `repairs/log.json`. A seed that moved less than 10% of the box by more than 60 levels is "no edit" and is not offered (`--min-change`, 0 keeps all); its raw redraw is still in `compare_<seed>.png`. A seed whose redraw differs from the panel on more than 3% of a 12 px band just outside the paste is flagged: the paste cuts that content off, so grow the box or the margin. A seed whose fine detail on the margin is under 0.7 or over 1.4 of the panel's is flagged as softer or sharper. Before generating, the command lists the strong lines in the panel that cross each edge of the paste.
- Placing the box: cover the whole limb or object with its reflection and shadow, because whatever continues outside the paste stays as drawn and breaks where the redraw moves it. Keep box edges off faces, and put them across flat areas (background, a plain sleeve) rather than through eyes, mouths or line-dense areas. The engine has no face detector to enforce this (the common licence-safe ones miss drawn faces), so the crossing list is the only automatic hint.
- Candidates and before/after crops land in `repairs/<image path>/rNN/`, and the run is logged with its beats, box, paste region, prompt, model, seeds and checks in `repairs/log.json`. `--gen-args` passes anything else to mflux (a Lightning LoRA, `--steps`). The mflux binaries are found through `"mflux": {"bin": "<venv>/bin"}` in `~/.motion-studio.json` (or `$MFLUX_BIN`), else on PATH. `"mflux": {"klein": ["--model", "<local 8-bit folder>", "--base-model", "flux2-klein-4b"]}` (and `"qwen": [...]`) replaces a model's default arguments; without it, the built-in names make mflux download the full official weights (about 15 GB for klein). `--image` works on a bare folder of panels with no `script.json`. The panel is untouched until `longform.py accept <film> panels/p07/r02 [--seed 2]`, which pastes that region onto the panel as it is now, with the colour match logged for that seed, so two repairs of one panel both survive. Repairs logged before this paste are accepted with the old one (feather outside the box). `revert` restores the panel from before a repair, newest first. After either, run `upscale` again; `timeline` flags the stale copy.
- Trade-offs: the model redraws the whole crop and only the paste is kept, so a seam can still show where line weight or shading differs (look at `compare_<seed>.png`). The wider crop for a small box gives the model more context, but the box is a smaller part of what it sees, so a small fix may need a more specific prompt. The 10% rule drops a real but small fix inside a large box; use a tighter box or `--min-change 0`. The colour match assumes the shift is the same across the crop; a redraw that relights only the box is not corrected. The checks are pixel statistics, not judgements of the drawing. A .jpg panel loses a little quality on each accept. Not used, for licence reasons: ADetailer and ultralytics (AGPL), MeshGraphormer and HandRefiner (they need MANO, which is non-commercial), OpenPose (non-commercial weights, which also rules out mflux's `--control pose:`).

**Gemini route** (paid; chosen by Bradley for K-factory's 8-episode test on 2026-10-19, about $70 in images). It draws panels with Nano Banana through the Gemini API instead of mflux, and it is the only paid step in the engine.
- `longform.py gemini-draw <film> [--beats b01 b02] [--jobs jobs.json] [--model flash|pro] [--now] [--redo] [--dry-run]` draws every panel that has no image yet. A panel's prompt, `refs` and `plate` come from script.json; `--jobs` takes a list of `{image, prompt, refs: [{path, role}], plate, model}` instead. Each reference's role is written into the prompt ("image 1 is Han's character sheet (face, hair, outfit), image 2 is the room plate"), which is what keeps Gemini from mixing up the references. A bare path gets a role from its file name, so give real roles. Panels are 16:9 at 1K (1376x768) by default. `upscale` then takes them to 2304x1296 with Real-ESRGAN. A panel's own `"size": "2K"` (in its script data or jobs entry) draws it at 2K instead. Use that for an extreme close-up pushed in past 1.4x, a panel with readable text, or a thumbnail frame. `--size` sets the size for panels that don't name one. Every call is stateless. `flash` is `gemini-nano-banana-2.1` (the default); `pro` is `gemini-3-pro-image`, and the per-panel `"model": "pro"` in a jobs file picks it for one hard panel. The default is the Batch API, at half price with results within 24 hours: the references are uploaded once through the Files API (reused for 24 hours), one batch job is made per model, and `longform.py gemini-collect <film> [--wait]` writes the panels when the job is done. `--now` draws interactively, at full price, for retries. `--dry-run` prints each prompt and its price and sends nothing. A panel that already exists is drawn again only with `--redo`, and the old one moves to `history/<path>/<time>.<ext>` first.
- `longform.py gemini-edit <film> --beat b40 --instruction "Move him up the bed so he sits against the carved headboard" --keep "the room" --keep "every face and outfit" [--ref plates/sickroom.png:"the room plate"]` edits a whole panel. For Gemini panels it replaces the crop repair. The result is a candidate in `repairs/<image>/rNN/`, logged like a crop repair, so `accept` and `revert` work the same way. The accepted panel takes Gemini's output size.
- Handed-back copies: Nano Banana sometimes returns an input unchanged. An output that matches an input's bytes, or moves under 0.5% of a thumbnail's pixels by more than 24 levels in any channel, is retried once in a fresh interactive call that starts "Generate a new image." If the retry is a copy too, nothing is saved and the panel is reported as flagged. A reference of another shape (a portrait character sheet) is not compared, because a copy keeps its shape.
- Sidecars: each output gets `<image stem>.gemini.json` beside it. It holds the model, the exact text sent, every reference with its role and sha256 (and the base image for an edit), the aspect and size, batch or not, the batch job, the tries, the price, the time, the panel it replaced, and the licence note. The licence note says that paid-tier output is usable commercially under Google's API terms, that Google claims no ownership, that there is no express licence, and that the output carries a SynthID watermark. Disclose AI content on YouTube where its rules require it.
- Spend cap: Google's budgets only send alerts, so the engine stops spending itself. Every call is priced before it is sent (per-image list price plus about 560 input tokens per image, as of 2026-10-19) and written to `~/.motion-studio/gemini-ledger.jsonl`. When a call returns, its price is settled from the reported token count. A run that would pass `"gemini": {"cap_usd": 15}` in `~/.motion-studio.json` is refused whole. So is one that would take the calendar month past `"month_cap_usd"`, which defaults to 80; `null` turns the monthly cap off. Each size is priced separately: on Nano Banana 2.1, 1K costs $0.0168 per image in batch ($0.0336 interactive) and 2K costs $0.0252 ($0.0504). A retry that would pass the cap is skipped and the panel is flagged. `longform.py gemini-spend` shows the totals. `"gemini": {"prices": {"<model>": {"2K": [standard, batch], "input": [standard, batch]}}}` updates the prices when Google changes them.
- Key and SDK: the key is read from `$GEMINI_API_KEY` or the macOS Keychain (`security add-generic-password -s motion-studio-gemini -a gemini -w`, which prompts for it), and it is never printed or written. The route needs `pip install google-genai` (Apache-2.0), imported only when a call is made.
- Trade-offs: this is the one paid step. 1K plus Real-ESRGAN costs about a third less than 2K ($1 an episode at 115 panels), but the upscaler sharpens lines rather than adding detail, which is why close-ups, text and thumbnails get 2K. The ledger is the engine's own count, not Google's bill; check the bill after the first run. A blocked or failed output is counted at $0, although Google may still bill its input tokens. The unchanged-output check catches copies, not instructions Gemini ignored while still changing the picture. The model names and prices are Google's as of 2026-10-19 and will change. Never script the consumer Gemini app instead: its terms forbid automated use, and the YouTube channels sit on the same Google account.

Write `"source"` on every segment that states a fact; it shows bottom left.

## Real things on screen

A scene that shows a real thing (a scam text, a company's site, a headline, an agency page) must show a capture of it. It must never show a convincing drawing of one. Captures are listed in `sources/captures.json`:

```json
{"ftc-toll-alert": {"file": "sources/ftc-toll-alert.png", "url": "https://consumer.ftc.gov/…", "captured": "2026-10-19",
                    "licence": "Public domain (US federal government work)", "credit": "FTC.gov"}}
```

`file`, `url`, `captured` (YYYY-MM-DD) and `licence` are required. For a page that isn't public domain, `licence` holds the fair-use note: commentary or news reporting, cropped to the part being discussed, credited. `credit` is optional. Show a capture with the `capture` scene, or point a drawn element at one with `"real": "<id>"`. Elements that look real are an `sms` scene, a stage beat with `notify` or `sms`, and a board card with a `web`, `doc`, `phone`, `bank` or `card` icon (a `kind: "note"` card is exempt). Each of these needs a `real` id or `"illustrative": true`. `illustrative` can go on the element, on the scene's `data`, or on the segment, and it is for things that are clearly made up, such as an example text written for the film. `timeline` and `render` stop and list every element that has neither marker, or whose capture is unlisted, missing a field or missing its file. There is no switch to skip the check.

This supports the Longtok rule: complaints are attributed to the agency or outlet that published them, so the capture is the attribution. Never name a company as a scam.

Trade-offs: every real-looking element needs either a capture or an explicit "made up" mark, so older scripts stop at `timeline` until they are marked. Most sites can't be reached from the cloud, so captures are taken on the Mac (a browser screenshot saved to `sources/`). A `real` id on a drawn `sms` records where the message came from, but the drawing is still a recreation; use `capture` to show the message itself.

## Captions

Long-form ships its captions as uploaded caption tracks, not burned in (decided 2026-10-17: the channel adds Spanish and Portuguese dubs as extra audio tracks on the same video, and burned English text would sit under them). `timeline` writes `out/captions.vtt` from the word times, with inner monologue in `<i>` and no speaker labels (no name badge is burned in either). For another language, write the translated lines as `{"<segment id>": "line"}` and run `longform.py captions <film> --lang es --text es.json`: each translation spans its segment's spoken words in the English read, split into pages of at most 42 characters that share the time by length, written to `out/captions.es.vtt`. It stops if a spoken segment has no line. `youtube.py captions <id> out/captions.es.vtt --language es --name Español` attaches a track. `"captions": true` still burns word-lit pages into the picture (about four words a page, spoken words turn from grey to ink, the current word is accent) for a cut that will never carry a dub. Shorts always burn them in (below). Anything else drawn as text in the picture (a panel's `caption_card`, a chart label) stays in English under every dub, so keep it to names, places and dates.

## Shorts

A Short is a 9:16 film re-laid-out from a run of a long-form film's segments. It is never a crop of the 16:9 render. `longform.py short <film> <short> --from s05 --to s09 [--title …]` makes a new film folder. It copies those segments' script entries, voice clips, word times, panel stills and `sources/`, and writes a `script.json` with `"format": "9x16"`, `"captions": true` and `"short_of"` (the long-form film and the segment range). It prints the Short's length and warns when it passes 3 minutes, YouTube's limit for a Short.

Then lay it out in the Short's own `script.json`, which is never overwritten. Put fewer things on screen at once (fewer list items, compare rows, board cards and hub spokes), give it its own camera (panel `focus` and `camera`, board card `x`/`y`, stage cast `x`), and cut text that the narration already says.

- **Layout.** Every scene type has a 9:16 layout. Text is larger and wraps narrower; `compare` stacks its columns with a rule between them; `flow` runs top to bottom with labels to the right; `bars` puts each label above its bar; `hub` becomes an upright oval; and the crowd pulls back until every seat fits; a `stage` holds its camera centred instead of leaning toward whoever acts, because a lean crops figures at the edges of an upright frame. Board card positions (0 to 1) map onto the safe area. The panel still keeps its 16:9 shape, so a 9:16 frame sees about a third of it, and `focus`/`camera` choose which third.
- **Safe area.** YouTube's Shorts controls cover the bottom ~22% and a right rail, so what must be read stays inside x 60–960 and y 220–1220. Captions sit just under that, at y 1260. The source line moves to the top, and there is no chapter label. These numbers are the long-form engine's own; `studio.py`'s 9x16 format still puts captions at y 1560.
- **Sound.** `timeline` checks that every segment lasts exactly as long as it does in the long-form film, then cuts the Short's `out/mix.wav` from the long-form mix: 0.3 s before the first line through the end of the last segment, a 0.4 s fade, then 0.8 s of silence. It also copies the long-form film's `sounds_used.json`, so `credits` works. Run `mix` on the long-form film first. If you changed `--pad` or `--lead` there, use the same values here, or `timeline` prints a PROBLEM. `mix` on a Short refuses unless you pass `--remix`.
- **Render resumes.** `render` draws the film in 30 s chunks and keeps every finished one in `out/parts`. A chunk that fails because the browser died or timed out (`TargetClosedError`) is retried up to `--retries` times (2) with half the workers. If it still fails, render stops and keeps the rest, and running it again renders only what is missing. Kept chunks are reused only while the page, timeline, assets and settings are unchanged, otherwise render starts over; `--fresh` forces that. Workers are capped by free memory at 1.5 GB each with 2 GB kept free (one worker measured about 1 GB on a plain film), and render says so when it lowers `--workers`. One dead worker at 6 workers cost a full hour before this (2026-10-24).
- **Page errors stop everything.** A JavaScript error in the film's page is the film's own code, so it fails the same way on every try. `render`, `stills` and every other frame grab stop at the first one and name the error, its file and line, the time and the segment. A render also stops its other chunks and is not retried, and the run exits non-zero. This came after a kit edit put a `//` comment mid-line, which commented out the next statement: every frame of one scene threw, and the render kept retrying that chunk without stopping or reporting (2026-10-26). Before that, page errors were only counted in a warning.
- **The page gate.** `check` first loads the page and seeks to the start, middle and end of every segment, and stops there if it sees any JavaScript error, `console.error` or unhandled promise rejection, listing each distinct one with its time and segment. It takes a second or two. `console.warn` and `console.log` are allowed. `render` runs the same gate before drawing anything. It also refuses to start unless the last `check` passed the gate on this exact version of the film: `review/check-gate.json` records `film_stamp`, the page, scripts and timeline by content and other files by size and time. `--skip-gate` renders anyway, prints why, and notes it in the status file, but the page gate itself still runs. Other check flags (text size, overlap, hold) don't block a render; they need a person's judgement. Trade-offs: a strict gate blocks a render over a harmless `console.error`, such as a kit logging a missing optional file. Fix the noise or log it with `console.warn`. The gate only seeks three moments per segment, so an error in between, like one that fires only mid-transition, gets through to the render, which then stops at the frame where it fires.
- **Status files.** `render`, `final` and `check` keep `out/<job>.status.json` up to date: `state` (running, done, failed), start and last-update times, progress (chunks done, for render), the last error, and notes such as a skipped gate. Each run ends with a single `DONE <job> <film>` or `FAILED <job> <film>: <reason>` line. A detached run's outcome can be read from the file or the log's last line without a live session. A run killed outright (power loss, `kill -9`) stays at `running`; compare `updated` with the clock.
- **Repeatable frames.** Every page the engine opens (render, stills, check) runs Chrome with `--disable-gpu-rasterization --disable-partial-raster` (`render.RASTER`). With Chrome's defaults, the same moment rendered twice gave different pixels on a page with gradients, filters and grain: on a Mac, 1 of 8 sampled moments differed in 1.82% of its pixels, and a fresh page's first frames differed by 1 to 3 levels. A re-rendered or resumed chunk would then not match the frames around it. With the flags, all 8 moments matched exactly (kit test film, 2026-10-26). `check` renders `--repeat` moments (8, spread through the film; 0 skips) twice, once more in the same page after seeking away and once in a fresh browser, and fails any that differ, with the share of pixels that changed. It costs a few seconds. `MS_GPU_RASTER=1` turns the flags off; check then names the setting next to any difference. Trade-offs: software raster rendered that 39 s gradient-heavy test about 25% slower on the Mac (0.43 s a frame against 0.35 s with one worker); the marker look is untimed. The probe samples 8 moments, so a fault that shows only in some scenes can still get through.
- **Reading time.** `check` lists every piece of on-screen text in `review/text-sheet.tsv` with when it is fully shown and how long it holds, and fails a block held under max(2 s, 1 s + words / 3), or under 3 s for a segment marked `"payoff": true` (its reveal is the point of the scene) or text still up when a chapter cuts. Lengthen the segment (`extra`), land the reveal on an earlier word, or cut words. See critic.md, text hold.
- **Review before render.** `render` on a 9:16 film stops until a `stills` contact sheet newer than `timeline.js` exists in `review/`. Look at it before rendering.

Trade-offs: each Short needs a layout and review pass instead of a free crop. A Short has to be a contiguous run of segments, because its sound is one cut from the long-form mix. A panel still is enlarged to 1920 tall, so a 9:16 panel is softer than the same panel in 16:9; draw panels meant for Shorts at 2K. The safe-area numbers are from YouTube's layout as of 2026-10 and may change.

## Music by mood

`music` measures every library track once (loudness, onset density, pulse strength, brightness, major/minor lean; stored in `catalog.json` as `mood`) and ranks tracks against each chapter's mood: `tension`, `mystery`, `serious`, `calm`, `warm`, `drive`, `uplift` (targets in `audio.MOODS`). Consecutive chapters with one mood share a track; a mood change brings a different track on a 3 s crossfade at the chapter line; the last track is back-timed so its own ending lands on the film's end. A run whose best fit is under 0.6 prints NO GOOD FIT with search words for the library source: fetch better tracks rather than shipping a club track under a tense chapter. Edit `track` in `out/music_plan.json` to override. Music sits at -25.5 dB under the voice (the channel owner found -24 a little loud) and ducks further while it speaks. Never put music with vocals under narration.

## UI sounds

`mix` adds one sound per on-screen event, read from the timeline: a buzz when a phone lights (`glow`), a ping when a message lands (`notify`, an `sms` beat or scene; 0.12 s after the buzz when both happen), a paper tick when a board card pins, a thud when a stamp hits. They sit at -12 dB (`--sfx-db`) under the voice. Same-kind sounds closer than the gaps in `SFX_GAP` are dropped so a burst of cards doesn't rattle. Each kind uses recorded library clips that carry `"role": "buzz" | "ping" | "pin" | "thud"` in `catalog.json` (cycled, level varied ±15%, transient on the cue). A film sets its own default per kind in script.json: `"ui_sounds": {"ping": false, "buzz": {"gain": 0.2, "lowpass": 2500}}` turns a kind off, or scales it (amplitude) and low-passes it (Hz, 4th-order Butterworth); the toll-text owner wanted no bell and a buzz that is felt more than heard. A kind with no recording stays silent and `mix` says so; `--sfx-drawn` fills it with a sound drawn in code, as a placeholder only: the channel owner liked that the sounds landed on their events in the toll-text film but found the drawn sounds themselves amateur. Sounds must be professional recordings or generations that are free for commercial use (sound.md).

## Thumbnails

Thumbnails are generated with Codex's built-in image tool under the owner's ChatGPT plan (`codex exec -i <reference.jpg>` with a written brief; no API key, so no per-image charge, but it draws on plan usage). Every image is checked for misspelt text, bad hands and a garbled phone before it is shown. Up to three go into YouTube's Test & compare: `out/meta.json` names them under `thumbnail_test`, and `thumbnail` names the one set at upload. Test & compare is set by hand in YouTube Studio on the desktop web, because the API can't set it, and it only runs while the video is public. The owner chose to test all three for the toll-text film (2026-10-23). Trade-offs: a test needs real public traffic before it says anything, and the winner is picked by YouTube on watch time, not clicks. The bottom-right corner stays clear of text and faces, because the timestamp sits there.

## Sharing a draft

`share` turns `out/<film>.mp4` into `out/share/index.html` (poster, chapter buttons, the script's palette accent) plus `out/share/v/`. Publish the page with the Artifact tool and every file in `v/` under its own path; the result is private until the owner shares it from the page's Share menu, and a viewer just presses play. Why it is built this way: the artifact host serves neither `.m3u8` nor `.ts`, caps each file at 15 MB and each publish at 64 MB, and loads scripts only from approved CDNs, so the cut goes out as fMP4 HLS segments named `.mp4` with the playlist renamed `index.txt`, played through hls.js from cdnjs. Segment length comes from the cut's average bitrate: about 10 MB of video at that rate, between 4 and 60 s, which leaves room for busier stretches and for each cut landing on the next keyframe. If a segment still comes out over 14 MB, `share` halves the length and splits again, up to four times. `--segment N` sets the starting length. A 10-minute 1080p cut is about 51 MB. Trade-off: a busy cut makes more, smaller files, so a long one can need more than one publish (it prints when it is over 60 MB). It needs Media Source Extensions: desktop browsers and iOS 17.1 or later; older iPhones get an error line. Optional `"share": {"headline", "lede", "eyebrow", "foot", "poster_at"}` in script.json sets the page text. The container's Playwright Chromium has no H.264 decoder, so a local test can only confirm that the page loads the playlist and segments, not that it plays; have the owner open it once before it goes to anyone.

## Lessons from the toll-text build

Each of these came from the channel owner watching a cut; the fix lives in the engine, not in one film.

- **Captions must follow the voice, not the page.** Long caption pages let the eye run ahead and word timing drifted up to 0.9 s after pauses. Fixed by short pages (about 4 words), word edges snapped to measured silence, and trying both readings of a year. Check any new voice on its first film: median word-start error should stay near 0.
- **Music under narration is quieter than it sounds while building.** -24 dB was too loud on a phone speaker; -25.5 dB is the default, with every track level-matched first so a quiet track doesn't jump at a chapter change.
- **Sound placement works; the sounds must be professional.** One sound per visible event, never per animation, from recorded or generated clips that are free for commercial use. Drawn-in-code sounds read as amateur.
- **A mood measure can't tell relative keys apart.** Composed minor beds read as major; trust chapter intent and override `out/music_plan.json` by hand.
- **Music sources may be blocked from the build machine.** Check the library can be reached before planning a film around new tracks (sound.md lists the routes).
- **The owner reviews on a link, not a file.** Run `share` with every draft so review doesn't wait on a download or a YouTube upload.

## Traps hit building this

- Two camera keys at the same time divide by zero and the camera jumps a whole frame. Build keys with `camKeys()`, which keeps them 0.8 s apart. It also caps camera speed: no glide averages more than 1300 screen px/s (`CAM_VMAX`; the eased peak is three times that), so a long move glides for up to 2.2 s (`CAM_GMAX`) instead of 0.9 s, and lands late when the gap since the last key is too short for it. Values are screen pixels, so a kit calling it at zoom z passes `vmax / z`. The Trapdoor kit's 2,400 px whip in 0.9 s smeared (2026-10-24). The engine's own scenes lean less than 800 px per key, so only kit cameras reach the cap.
- An opaque scene that doesn't paint every pixel shows the previous scene through its gaps until that one leaves, which reads as a pop. Opaque scenes get a full-frame backdrop automatically; keep it if you write a new one.
- Fixed labels on a scene with a moving camera drift off frame: put them in `s.ov`, the per-segment overlay that the camera doesn't move.
- **Text shimmered under the camera.** A slow zoom (the 1.0 to 1.03 drift every scene had, and the stage, board and screenshot pushes) re-rasterises every glyph each frame, so line gaps jittered by up to 2 px. The owner saw it as bubble text "blinking" on its line spacing; a screenshot can't show it. Fixed by dropping zoom from every scene except the drama `panel` (whose text sits in `s.ov`), landing unscaled pans on whole pixels, and letting springs (`K.step`) settle to exactly 1. Camera leans toward the newest card, speaker or mark remain, as whole-pixel pans. Check text stability by measuring line gaps across consecutive frames, not by eye on stills.
- `verify` treats a wipe crossing a bright figure, or a stamp, as a pop. `final` passes those windows as allowed; anything outside them is a real glitch.
