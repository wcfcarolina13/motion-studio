# Figures (figure.js)

Flat, rigged people drawn in SVG, posed as a pure function of t. Load after kit.js. They are ours (no licensed art), so they can be used anywhere.

```js
const you = F.figure(parent, { skin: 2, hair: "short", hairColor: 1, shirt: K.C.ACCENT, face: "right" });
K.run(t => F.place(you, 700, 900, 540, F.idle(t, 1, F.at(t, [[0, "stand"], [2.1, "phone"], [4.0, "worried"]])), { glow: 0.8 }));
```

- `F.figure(parent, opts)`: `skin` 0–4 or a colour, `hair` (`short long bun curly cap hood bald`), `hairColor` 0–4 or a colour, `shirt`, `pants`, `shoes`, `face` (`right`/`left`), `kind: "silhouette"` for a person the story must not identify (a flat grey shape with no face).
- `F.place(fig, x, groundY, height, pose, {face, prop, glow})` draws it and returns `{head, hand}` screen points for bubbles and props.
- Poses (`F.POSES`): `stand relaxed phone read worried shocked shrug point present think angry happy cheer type sit sitphone slump realise`. A pose is joint angles plus face settings (`brow` -1 angry … 1 worried, `mouth` -1 frown … 1 smile, `open`, `look`, `lookY`), so `F.blend(a, b, p)` mixes any two, and you can pass a literal `{rs: 80, re: 10, mouth: 0.5}`.
- `F.at(t, [[t, pose], …], dur, {anticipate})` springs between poses. With `anticipate`, a big change (arms, head and lean moving more than 80 degrees together) first leans the other way for 0.12 s, ending on the key so the move still starts on its word: 12% of the change, at most 10 degrees a joint. The body and limbs then overshoot by about 5% and settle; face settings never overshoot. A key's third element `{hold: n}` freezes the pose for n frames at the top of its overshoot, an accent on a realisation; `realise` holds 2 frames by default. With `anticipate`, the key (or the call's options) can also set the spring: `z`, its damping (0.68; lower overshoots more), and `w`, its speed (13; lower is a slower creep or sink), e.g. `[3, "slump", {w: 6}]`. `F.keyer(spec)` builds the same timeline for any rig whose poses are flat objects of numbers; `F.closeup` uses it. Without `anticipate` the move lands on the target with no lean back, overshoot or hold. Until 2026-10-25 the overshoot was clamped away by mistake, so every film made before then has none. Stage scenes turn it on with the film's `anticipate` (below). `F.idle(t, seed, pose)` adds breathing, a sway and a blink every few seconds. `F.walk(phase, pose)` is a walk cycle (phase in steps; about 2.4 steps a second reads as a normal walk). Don't switch a figure straight onto `F.walk`: its legs jump to mid-stride on the first frame. `F.stride(t, a, b, pose)` walks from time a to b, starting from the first step and easing the stride in over the first ~0.35 s and back out into the held pose before b; stage scenes use it for entrances and `move` beats.
- `F.crowd(parent, n, seed)` makes n varied people for crowd shots. They are marked as background people, which `check`'s idle-figure test leaves out; `opts.id` on F.figure or F.closeup's figure names a character in its flags.

Craft notes: keep the protagonist in the accent colour and everyone else in muted shirts, so the eye finds them. Change pose on the spoken word (a `stage` beat with `at`), and keep a held pose alive with `F.idle`. Use silhouettes for criminals and anonymous actors, never a face or skin colour that could read as a group of people being blamed.

## Close-ups (closeup.js)

`F.closeup` is a second character for close shots: a head and two stubby hands peeking over an edge (a phone, a desk, the bottom of the frame), acting through big face parts. It has no body. `init` copies closeup.js and the long-form template loads it after figure.js, so a kit layer or a hand-built scene can draw one. It came from a kit layer (2026-10-26), where a 10 s test at 8 Mbps showed no grain change and no pops, and the port gives byte-identical poses and SVG to the kit's version. `examples/closeup/index.html` plays it on a loop with a scrubber.

```js
const me = F.closeup.figure(g, { skin: "#ff7a78", hair: "tuft", glasses: ["halfmoon", "arch", "slit"], front: frontG });
// per frame:
F.closeup.place(me, 960, 820, 420, F.closeup.idle(t, 3, F.closeup.at(t, [[0, "hidden"], [1.2, "peek"], [2, "up", { hold: 14 }]])));
```

- `F.closeup.figure(parent, opts)`: `skin` (a paint, so a gradient works), `skinShade` (ears, nose), `hair` (`tuft`, `fringe`, `none`), `hairColor`, `hand`, `handShade`, `glasses` (`"none"`, or a list of lens names or paths, morphed in order by the pose's `lens`), `frame`, `white`, `pupilColor`, `mouthColor`, `tongue`, `cheek`, `facing` (`front`, `left`, `right`), and `front`: a group the caller puts in front of whatever forms the edge, for the fingertips that grip it. An unknown lens name stops the page with "closeup: unknown lens".
- `F.closeup.place(fig, x, y, h, pose, {facing})`: (x, y) is the middle of the edge and h the head's height in px. Everything behind the edge is clipped to it, so `hidden` shows nothing. Returns `{head, hands}` points for bubbles and props.
- `F.closeup.at(t, keys, dur, o)` is `F.at` for this rig (via `F.keyer`) with `anticipate` on unless `o` turns it off: a big change moves 12% the other way first, then the head and hands overshoot and settle, face settings and grips never overshoot, and nothing dips below the edge in the counter-move. The reaction poses (`worried`, `shocked`, `realise`, `suspicious`, `relief`) hold 14 frames at the overshoot unless the key says otherwise. `z` and `w` work as in `F.at`.
- Poses: `hidden`, `peek`, `up`, `worried`, `shocked`, `realise`, `suspicious`, `relief`. Values: `rise` (0 hidden to 1 up), `tilt`, `slide`, `squash`, each hand's rise, x and grip (`hlRise`, `hlX`, `hlGrip`, `hrRise`, `hrX`, `hrGrip`), `brow`, `mouth`, `open`, `look`, `lookY`, `blink`, `pupil`, `lens`. `F.closeup.pose(name, over)` changes some values; `F.closeup.blend(a, b, p)` mixes two.
- `F.closeup.idle(t, seed, pose)` adds breath, a blink every few seconds and settling hands, deterministic per seed.
- Lenses: `F.closeup.LENSES` has `halfmoon`, `arch`, `slit` and `oval` (the plain eye with no glasses). The morph is a plain lerp of path numbers, which is exact for shapes with the same point structure. `F.closeup.lens(inside)` makes a lens from any star-shaped region around the eye centre (`(x, y) => true` inside, in head units) with that shared structure. A path that doesn't share it snaps at the halfway mark, and the page warns ("closeup: lenses 1 and 2 have different point structures"), which `render` and `check` print as LINT.
- Trade-offs: without glasses the eye is the single `oval`, so `lens` does nothing and `suspicious` doesn't narrow the eyes. The kit's GSAP morph path was left out; the built-in lenses gave the same shape to 0.0001 head units without it, and the engine stays free of GSAP.
