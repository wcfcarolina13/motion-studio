## Self-check before boarding

- [ ] Line 1 is a person, a physical verb and a concrete object, under 14 words, with no setup.
- [ ] A cold reader of line 1 alone asks "why?" and guesses wrong. Write their guess here: …
- [ ] Each line before the turn can be drawn as a single still. None of them names a feeling.
- [ ] No reason before the turn: no "because", "so that", "in order to", "to make sure", "the reason" or "turns out".
- [ ] Each escalation raises the cost, the risk or the strangeness, and none of them explains.
- [ ] The reason re-explains line 1, not just the last action.
- [ ] The close shows line 1's object or place again, with no moral and no question.
- [ ] Fiction: nothing says "true", "real" or "based on". True story: every line has a source below.

## Worked example (fiction, house-written)

Thesis: the ticket was the way out she gave up, kept so her daughter would know that staying was a choice. About a mother keeping a secret, not a mother keeping proof. 116 words.

| # | Part | Sentence | Cut on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Act | A mother sewed a folded bus ticket inside her daughter's school coat. | sewed |
| 2 | Escalation | Every winter she unpicked the lining and moved the ticket to the new coat. | moved |
| 3 | Escalation | When the coat tore at the shoulder, she mended it around the ticket. | mended |
| 4 | Escalation | When her daughter left for Seoul, she sewed it into the suitcase. | sewed |
| 5 | Escalation | Her daughter found it at twenty-six, cutting a loose thread on the train. | found |
| 6 | Escalation | It was a one-way ticket, dated the day she was born, never stamped. | never |
| 7 | Turn | You see, her mother had bought it to leave, the night before the birth. | leave |
| 8 | Turn | She kept the ticket she didn't use, so her daughter would know she chose to stay. | stay |
| 9 | Close | The daughter sewed it back inside her own coat. | sewed |
